# Part 6: app vs no app, measured again: pre-registration

Written 2026-10-19, before any of the six books exists. Do not edit this file after results arrive;
the results go in a separate file.

Part 6 of `run-10-plan.md`. Parts 1–5 are merged (#622–#627), and so are the fixes from writer
run 11 (#628). The app is at `4194f86`.

## What changed from the first comparison

The first comparison (`run-10-plan.md`, *Where it comes from*) had one app book against two
controls. Its own caveats were:
- one book per side;
- different instructions;
- an app writer who was also reporting bugs.

This run fixes all three:
- **Three books a side.**
- **The same brief, word for word, for all six writers.** The only difference is a section on how
  to work: in Kathala, or in plain files. Both sides end the same way, by reading the book back,
  revising it, and putting it in one `book.md` in the same format.
- **No bug-reporting duty.** All six writers are the same general-purpose agent. In run 10 the
  app writer was the `writer-review` agent, whose job includes reporting the app's problems. Here
  the app writers are told to work around anything in the way and to write nothing about the app.

The brief is the run-10 brief: a gritty space opera in 21 chapters, three eras centuries apart,
interlaced, with synchronised pacing and no era spoiling another.

## The books

- **App books (A1, A2, A3):** written in Kathala at `4194f86`, each in its own browser profile and
  dev server.
- **Control books (C1, C2, C3):** written in plain files, each in its own folder, with no access
  to anything outside it.
- **Waves:** they are written in two waves of three, A1, C1 and A2, then C2, A3 and C3, so that a
  usage limit falls on both sides alike.
- **Before evaluation:**
  - Each `book.md` is checked for the format, and anything an app leaves in prose is removed:
    a scene-header line, a `##` scene title. Each removal is listed in the results.
  - The six books are then given shuffled letters, and the mapping is sealed until all judging
    is done.

## Measures (as in the first comparison)

1. **Verified continuity errors.**
   - Two independent auditors per book use the run-10 audit prompt.
   - I check every claimed error against the text under the run-10 rules:
     - **accept** the book's own facts clashing;
     - **reject** a character's estimate, rounding, rhetoric or belief, in-world myth, and
       anything ambiguous or explainable in one line.
   - Quotes are checked mechanically (`quotes.py`) before anything else.
   - **Score:** verified errors per 10,000 words, all severities and serious + minor.
2. **Blind pairwise judging.** The run-10 judge prompt, unchanged.
   - Each judge reads both books in full and scores six things out of 10:
     interlacing, synchronised pacing, no spoilers, coherence, craft and overall.
   - Each judge then names a winner and the strength of the preference.
   - **App vs control:** all 9 pairs, each judged twice with the order swapped. That is 18
     judgements.
   - **Control vs control:** all 3 pairs, both orders. That is 6 judgements. They measure how far
     judgements move by chance, between books made the same way.
   - **App vs app:** not judged. It does not bear on the question.

## The question and the decision rule (fixed now)

**Did the app books move from worst to within the controls' range, or above it?**

A judgement of "equal" counts half to each side. "App wins" means the share of the 18
app-vs-control judgements won by the app book.

- **Above the controls.** All of the following:
  - the app wins at least 12 of 18;
  - the app books' mean verified error rate (all severities) is no higher than the controls';
  - the app books' mean "no spoilers" score is no lower than the controls'.
- **Within the controls' range.** The app wins 6 to 11.5 of 18, and neither side's mean error
  rate is more than 1.5× the other's.
- **Still worst.** The app wins at most 5.5 of 18, or the app books' mean error rate is more
  than 1.5× the controls'.
- **Anything else** is reported as mixed, with the numbers.

The control-vs-control judgements are read alongside these:
- if the controls split their own pairs close to evenly, a 12-of-18 result is a real difference;
- if one control wins every judgement against the others, books differ a lot by chance, and
  that is said beside the result.

Secondary, reported but not part of the rule:
- the mean of each of the six judge scores, per side;
- each book's word count;
- each app book's use of the app: facts with spoiler words, checker runs, AI audits.
  These are read from its `.pwk` after judging.

**Caveat:** three books a side is still a small sample. The result is evidence about this model
and this brief, not proof.
