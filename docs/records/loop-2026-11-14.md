## How it was run

The same two agents, the same prompts as the second loop (`loop-2026-11-12.md`), a new editor's name and a new book.
- **The writer** chose the pen name Tamsin Hale. *The Salt Ledger* is a fantasy mystery: 6 chapters, 17 scenes and
  12,496 words as first sent, 7 chapters, 18 scenes and 13,806 at the end. It is the third run in a row whose writer
  chose that title. The book, its town and its cast are new.
- **The editor** was Maren Hollis, working in LibreOffice with tracking on.
- **The rounds.** As the second loop: the writer wrote and sent the book; the editor edited it and wrote a letter;
  the writer brought that in, revised and sent it back marked; the editor edited again; the writer did the same; the
  editor made a last pass; the writer brought that in and finished.
- **The prompts did not say** what was measured, or that the marked file or the clean copy exists. The writer found
  both in the export dialog, ticked by default, and kept them each time.
- **Blindness.** As before. The writer opened `ux-review.md` and the round-trip plans only at the end.
- **Interruptions.**
  - A usage limit stopped the editor once, early in round 3, after reading the writer's note and before any edit.
    The editor was resumed and redid nothing.
  - The app's server reached its time limit twice and was restarted from the same frozen build: once while the
    editor worked, once after the writer's round 4. The writer checked the book on reopening: all there.
  - The writer's own script, in round 1, closed the browser within a second of an edit and lost a chapter's retitle.
    It was retyped.
- **One file was edited by hand.** LibreOffice driven by script cannot make a threaded reply. In rounds 2 and 3 the
  editor added the missing parent links (`w15:paraIdParent`, 7 and then 2) to `commentsExtended.xml` after each save,
  and nothing else. An editor clicking Reply would have sent the same thing. The unpatched files are kept.

Accept All gave the clean copy, and Reject All gave the editor's own text. What the file did not say:
- **which of the editor's edits stand.** They come back accepted into the text, as D-4 has them, and only those
  turned back are marked. The writer's note said *66*, *4 turned back*, and then listed seven. To know, the editor
  wrote a classifier that checked each of the 67 against the clean copy: 55 stand, 7 not taken, 5 in passages since
  rewritten;

  (finding 1);
- **what changed in the book's shape.** The new chapter is not marked (finding 2);
- **where eight comments had been** (finding 4).

## New findings, for the next part

Ranked by what they cost. *Confirmed here* means checked against the code or the files after the run.

### A split scene

1. **A split scene goes back as deleted and written again.** The writer's W3-2 and the editor's round-2 measure.
   - The writer split the last scene of chapter 6, and moved its second half into a new chapter 7. In the marked
     file, 150 words that were not changed are struck under the first scene and put in again under the second:
     *"Now it adds."* twice, and the editor's *"Perfect last line. Don't touch it."* on the struck copy.
   - **Confirmed here.** `markedPlan` marks each scene against the same scene in the editor's file
     (`markedParagraphs(base.get(s.eventId), …)`). A scene made by a split has no base: all of it is new. The words
     taken from the first are gone from it.
   - The app's own Compare said it right: *Split from "The Magistrate"*. The writer wrote a paragraph of the cover
     note to say the ending had not been rewritten.
2. **A new chapter, its title and a new scene break are not marked.** The editor's F-2, the writer's W3-4.
   - The heading paragraphs are written afresh in every export and carry no mark; the `#` they replaced carries no
     deletion. Reject All leaves the new chapter in place.
   - The editor: *"Chapter Seven appears as if it had always been there."*
3. **A split records nobody in the new scene.** The writer's W3-1, reproduced in the book and a scratch world, with a
   control.
   - The new scene's header shows the place and cast it was split from. Change the place of the scene before, and
     everyone in the new scene is there too, against what its header says.
   - **Confirmed here.** `splitScene` gives the new scene its place and cast fields (`splitCarries`) and writes no
     record of anyone there. A pasted or typed header does write one; the guide promises a header *keeps them on the
     Quay whatever an earlier scene is later changed to say*.
   - Nothing on the page says so. Retyping a letter of the Place line puts it right.

6. **Every tracked change, and the app's line, carries the export's date.** The editor's F-4 and F-3.
