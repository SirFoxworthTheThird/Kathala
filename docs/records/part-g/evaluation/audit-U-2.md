# Continuity audit: book-U

1. **Teague is too old to have been nineteen in the strike of '64**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 10 (and 19)
   - Ch. 1 (FY 2291): "the gang's tether man, sixty-three years old"
   - Ch. 4: "Mother Szell who was supposed to have been on the Ostrava docks in the strike of '64 alongside Teague"
   - Ch. 10: "We did it on the Ostrava docks in '64 ... I was one of the ones they didn't hang. I was nineteen." (Ch. 19 repeats it: "Teague at nineteen walking under them every day of his life.")
   - Why both can't be true: the year is 2291, so the only possible '64 is 2264, 27 years earlier. A man of 63 was 36 then, not 19. If he was 19 in 2264 he is 46 now.
   - Severity: **minor**

2. **The Ring is 38 km across in one place and 38 km round in another**
   - Chapters: 1, 15, 19, 21
   - Ch. 1: "The Ring. Thirty-eight kilometers across"
   - Ch. 19: "The whole inner face of it, thirty-eight kilometers round"; Ch. 21: "The gold lines on its face, all the way round, thirty-eight kilometers of them"
   - Ch. 15: "clamped on, thirty-one kilometers round the rim from the Spindle"
   - Why both can't be true: a ring 38 km across is about 119 km round. If it were 38 km round, nowhere on it could be more than 19 km from the Spindle along the rim, so the 31-km crash site and the seven-day walk could not happen.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **Riggers from Gangs Nine and Eleven die in the Barn, but they never went there**
   - Chapters: 16, 19
   - Ch. 16: "There were six of them on the *Patience*, in the end. Sol. Bracken ... Pell ... Oyelaran, for the crane. Haddad. And Juno." (Juno stays at the tug's controls.)
   - Ch. 16, later: "There had been three riggers dead in the Barn besides him, from the Proctors' guns. Two from Gang Nine. One from Eleven, the boy who had said *that's mutiny* in the ballast hold."
   - Ch. 19 relies on those deaths: "There were four hundred and six of them" (412 less Teague, Bracken, the three Barn dead and Sol).
   - Why both can't be true: the only people in the Barn are Sol, Bracken, Pell, Oyelaran and Haddad. No other tug or party of riggers is ever sent, so two more Gang Nine riggers and the Gang Eleven boy cannot have died there.
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **One of Hold Fourteen's lungs goes missing**
   - Chapters: 9, 15
   - Ch. 9: "There had been four lungs in Hold Fourteen when I was born. One had died when I was nine." Then Number Two dies: "That's Number Two."
   - Ch. 9, Veska: "We've got one lung, Tally. One. Number Three."
   - Ch. 15: "Number Three died four days after ... The whole Hold's breathing the black."
   - Why both can't be true: four lungs, minus one at age nine, minus Number Two, leaves two. The book treats Number Three as the last one, and no other loss is ever mentioned.
   - Severity: **minor**

5. **Sallow says the Keepers no longer take Sump novices, but one is serving now**
   - Chapters: 8, 14 (and 17, 20)
   - Ch. 8: "a long-necked boy from the Sump named Tobiah who had been with the Keepers six months"; "He was sixteen, and from the Sump, and he had been a Keeper six months"
   - Ch. 14, Sallow: "I was a novice from the Sump ... Most of us were, once. They've stopped taking us now."
   - Why both can't be true: the narration says plainly that the order took a Sump boy as a novice six months ago. He is not hiding where he is from, and he is still a novice in Chs. 17 and 20.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **Days left to Firstlight: twenty-four or twelve, in the same scene**
   - Chapters: 11 (with 14 and 17 agreeing with the shorter count)
   - Ch. 11, Sallow: "in twenty-four days it will all be over"; Idris, the same night: "In twenty-four days they're going to throw the Drum away"
   - Ch. 11, Lark, later that night: "Benno's going to the pens. Day after tomorrow. That's when they take the drawn. Ten days before Firstlight they go into the Levy pens"
   - Ch. 14 puts the gallery visit six days after the Instrument ("He had thought about it for six days"). Ch. 17, on Firstlight Eve, puts it six days earlier still ("You've had a face on you for six days. Since you went up the gallery that night"). That makes about twelve days in all.
   - Why both can't be true: if the pens open the day after tomorrow and they open ten days before Firstlight, Firstlight is twelve days away, not twenty-four. The later day counts also give about twelve.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **When Idris resealed the grille: six days before Firstlight Eve, or thirteen nights before Firstlight?**
   - Chapters: 17, 20
   - Ch. 17 (Firstlight Eve): "You've had a face on you for six days. Since you went up the gallery that night." Sallow: "I've thought about what you said. Up there, in the gallery. All week."
   - Ch. 20 (Firstlight morning): "He cut the lead seals he had set himself thirteen nights ago"
   - Why both can't be true: six days before the Eve is seven nights before Firstlight morning, not thirteen.
   - Severity: **minor**

8. **Idris's age doesn't fit his memory of Lark as a baby**
   - Chapters: 2, 17
   - Ch. 2: Idris first saw the Knot "at fifteen, a novice"; Lark is "seventeen"
   - Ch. 17, Sallow: "I taught you the invocation ... You were fifteen ... I've been sorry for it for nineteen years." That makes Idris about 34, seventeen years older than Lark.
   - Ch. 17: "Idris had loved it as a child. He had stood on a chair ... with his father's hand on his shoulder, and Lark in his mother's arms, very small"
   - Why both can't be true: with a seventeen-year gap, Idris was about seventeen, and already a novice, when Lark was a baby. He was not a child standing on a chair.
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **The Clemency's date doesn't match Sallow's forty-eight days**
   - Chapters: 5, 17
   - Ch. 5 (the day after Levy Day), Sallow: "You have forty-eight days." Then "It took him four days to find the right one", and Oake: "A ship is coming through the Eye in eleven days."
   - Ch. 17, Severs: "You falsified an entry in the Transit Register on the thirty-seventh day before Firstlight. The *Clemency*."
   - Why both can't be true: about fifteen days after Sallow's forty-eight-day warning leaves about 33 days to Firstlight, not 37. (Ch. 11's own "eleven days" plus "twenty-four days" agrees with Severs, which makes Ch. 5 the outlier.)
   - Severity: **trivial**

10. **Sol's day marks: 38 or 37 left?**
    - Chapter: 10
    - "Thirty-eight. Thirty-eight days, still, to Day Three Hundred."
    - "the place in the rows where Day Two Eighty-Eight would be—twenty-five marks along from the last one she had crossed"
    - Why both can't be true: both counts start from the same last crossed mark. If Day 288 is 25 marks along, Day 300 is 25 + 12 = 37 marks along, not 38. (On the night of Day 263 the plain arithmetic, 300 − 263, also gives 37.)
    - Severity: **trivial**

11. **Dog Number One: driven home "two days ago" was three days ago**
    - Chapters: 16, 19
    - Ch. 16: the dog is seated on the night of the mutiny, Day 284 ("the twelfth dog came out yesterday", and the dogs were pulled on Day 283)
    - Ch. 19, on the evening of Day 287, after the *Assessor* docks "at the start of second shift on Day Two Hundred and Eighty-Seven": "Number One's dog stood out of its cuff a hand's width, where they had driven it home two days ago"
    - Why both can't be true: Day 284 to Day 287 is three days.
    - Severity: **trivial**

12. **Lark says the cord was on her wrist "two days ago", but it was cut that afternoon**
    - Chapter: 8
    - "A Guard came to the rooms ... in the middle of the afternoon ... and cut the red cord from her wrist". That evening she leaves, and Idris follows "in the middle of the night".
    - At C-14 that same night, Lark: "This was on my wrist two days ago. It's on his now."
    - Why both can't be true: the cord was cut the same day, only hours earlier.
    - Severity: **trivial**

13. **A 900-metre stair that ends three-quarters of the way up a 900-metre tower**
    - Chapters: 2, 20, 21
    - Ch. 2: "The Spindle rose nine hundred meters off the rim ... and the gallery was a band of glass three-quarters of the way up"
    - Ch. 21: the stair "goes up from the door in the foot, through the whole height of the tower, nine hundred meters ... At the top the stair ended in a door ... we came out into the gallery ... three-quarters of the way up the Spindle". (Ch. 20 likewise has "nine hundred meters of stair" from the chapter-house to the gallery.)
    - Why both can't be true: the gallery sits about 675 m up. A stair through the whole 900 m tower cannot end at it. (The 900 m could be read as the length of a spiral, but Ch. 21 calls it "the whole height of the tower".)
    - Severity: **trivial**

Counts by severity: serious 0, minor 8, trivial 5 (13 in total).
