# Continuity audit: book-Q

1. **Basu built the Tally at 26, but the Tally is ninety years old**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 19 (and 21)
   - Ch 1: "the Tally, which has kept you safe for ninety years". Ch 4 (Achterberg, with Basu in the room): "It was built, ninety years ago, to make sure no one on this rock ever took air that was not paid for."
   - Ch 4: Basu is "A small, neat, brown man of fifty or so", and says "The Tally can't be unlocked without stopping it. I built it so. I was twenty-six and very good at my work". Ch 19: "I made it the first year," he said. "When I built her. I was twenty-six" and "I've thought about that every night for twenty-six years." Ch 21 has the Tally keeping accounts "for eight hundred years", which fits a build around 2296.
   - Why both can't be true: a man of about fifty-two who built the system at twenty-six built it about 26 years ago, not 90. Who built the Tally, and so who could have planted the back door, drives the climax.
   - Severity: **serious**

2. **What a child's debt costs per year**
   - Chapters: 1, 4
   - Ch 1: "the ordinary minus three hundred that a Lantern child had by eight from being fed and housed and breathed."
   - Ch 4 (Hesketh): "You've given us six hundred credits of rope, Director. That's a year for a child."
   - Why both can't be true: at 300 credits in eight years, 600 credits would cover about sixteen years of a child's keep, not one. (Ch 1 also prices a meal at four credits, which fits Hesketh's figure better.)
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **Which place on Lantern has no camera**
   - Chapters: 4, 7 (10)
   - Ch 4 (Basu, who "designed the camera net"): "There's only one place on Lantern the Tally doesn't watch. The Wick's too bright for the lenses."
   - Ch 7 (narration): "They met in the old chapel on Gallery Four, because it was the only room on Lantern with no Company camera in it." In Ch 10 Basu himself picks the chapel for his confession.
   - Why both can't be true: the man who designed the camera net says the Wick is the only unwatched place. The narrative says the chapel is unwatched too.
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **Joss's position on the Wick during the strike**
   - Chapter: 7
   - "There were forty collimators on the Wick, ring after ring of them down the eleven-kilometre length of the lamp"; "Dagny took the master ring at the root of the lamp... Joss had the middle."
   - "He was on the middle ring, a hundred metres along, with two of his crew; he had his big arms wrapped round the housing of the twentieth collimator and his face turned toward her, and he was grinning".
   - Why both can't be true: the twentieth of forty collimators spread along 11 km is about 5.5 km from the root, not a hundred metres. At that distance Dagny could not see him grin.
   - Severity: **minor**

5. **Fen says the bell's names were cut for three hundred years**
   - Chapters: 6 (against 1, 3, 7)
   - Ch 6 (Fen): "Somebody cut every one of these by hand, for three hundred years, so that the people who died on that rock wouldn't just be numbers".
   - Ch 3 (Fen herself): the *Ardent Mercy* "crossed into the Sabra system in 2296", and "The Throat closed in 2387". Ch 1: "among nine decades of brown". Ch 7: "Ninety-one years she's hung there."
   - Why both can't be true: the bell was in use for 91 years before it left Lantern. Fen is the expert who gave those dates, so this is not a character's mistake.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **When the fake closing phase was "recorded"**
   - Chapters: 1, 10
   - Ch 1 (announcement): "Three weeks ago the station recorded the beginning of what our physicists call a closing phase."
   - Ch 10 (Basu): "The notice came in on the data-run three weeks before the Director's announcement... Three weeks later, the throat-watch recorded the onset of a closing phase."
   - Why both can't be true: by Basu's account the closing phase was claimed three weeks after a notice that itself came three weeks before the announcement. That puts the claim on the day of the announcement. The Director dated it three weeks before the announcement, the same time the notice arrived.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **Dacey listened for four days by the third day**
   - Chapters: 9 (6)
   - Ch 9: "The signal came on the third day out from the Throat". Then Dacey "had been listening to the silence of the Sabra system for four days". Ch 6 has him listening only "since the red began to thin".
   - Why both can't be true: by the third day after leaving the Throat he cannot have been listening to the system for four days.
   - Severity: **trivial**

8. **Dagny's margin above the line: 611 or 11?**
   - Chapter: 13
   - "I'm in credit. Just. I've got six hundred and eleven."
   - The transfer is refused, and then she gives Pim the ration "that the Tally still issued to her because she was still, by eleven credits, on the right side of the line".
   - Why both can't be true: no transfer happened, so her margin is either 611 or 11, not both. "Just" fits 11.
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **Tamsin's sum for four cassettes**
   - Chapters: 14 (and 11, 20)
   - Ninian, Ch 14: "Each one burns about a third of her length for three years, or the whole of her for one. Fourteen... would light her end to end... For fourteen years." Ch 11 gives the same rates.
   - Tamsin, same chapter: "Four's a year at full length," said Tamsin. "Barely three at a third."
   - Why both can't be true: at one year per cassette at full length, four cassettes last four years at full length and about twelve at a third. Nobody corrects the figure, and Wenna answers it as if it were right.
   - Severity: **minor**

10. **Marisol Tey's daughter's age**
    - Chapters: 12, 15
    - Ch 12 (pod card, 3089): "Dependants: one (1), female, b. 3086, retained Kessane, attaching on majority."
    - Ch 15 (Marisol): "It attaches on majority. She's six."
    - Why both can't be true: a child born in 3086 is about three in 3089, not six. Marisol is dying and confused, but the book does not make the mismatch a point.
    - Severity: **minor**

11. **How long Ode has run throats**
    - Chapters: 3, 12, 18
    - Ch 3 (Ode): "The captain's had her hold sealed by a lien-house for the first time in thirty years of running throats".
    - Ch 12 (Ode): "I've run throats for thirty-four years". Ch 18: "Thirty-four years," she said.
    - Why both can't be true: her own career length changes by four years over a few weeks of voyage.
    - Severity: **trivial**

12. **How many bailiffs Kell has**
    - Chapters: 3, 18
    - Ch 3: "Ten men and women were coming up the dock in a loose column... At their head was a heavy, unhurried man of fifty with a sergeant's flash".
    - Ch 18 (Haste): "Sergeant Kell's people are very good, but there are twelve of them."
    - Why both can't be true: the team is ten, or eleven with Kell, not twelve. No one joins between Ambry and the limb.
    - Severity: **trivial**

13. **"Eighteen hours" to the limb, said twice about twelve hours apart**
    - Chapters: 18, 21
    - Ch 18, morning: "We clear the limb in eighteen hours," Haste said.
    - Ch 18, that night: "in eighteen hours the *Good Title* would clear the limb". Ch 21: they clear it around six the next morning, after Corvin has read "Since about midnight".
    - Why both can't be true: if the limb was eighteen hours off in the morning, it was not still eighteen hours off that night. The clearing at about 6 a.m. matches the morning figure.
    - Severity: **trivial**

14. **When Dagny last saw the bell**
    - Chapters: 16, 19
    - Ch 16: "They took the bell down at two in the morning" after the last name was cut "at eleven at night on the forty-seventh day". So that is the early hours of day 48, before the 7:05 root attempt.
    - Ch 19 (about 4–5 a.m. on day 49): "She had seen it last at two in the morning, two days ago".
    - Why both can't be true: about 27 hours had passed, so it was the previous night, not two days ago.
    - Severity: **trivial**

15. **"Three nights before": opening the frame-40 hatch**
    - Chapters: 17, 20
    - Ch 17: the top hatch is opened "at two in the morning on the twenty-third day".
    - Ch 20: "They went up the keel on the twenty-seventh day... It took all night. ... At four in the morning". Orrin opens the lower hatch "because he had opened its twin in the floor of the fifth hall three nights before".
    - Why both can't be true: from the night of the 22nd–23rd to the night of the 27th is four or five nights, not three.
    - Severity: **trivial**

16. **How many of the Called walked the keel: four or five thousand**
    - Chapters: 11, 17, 20
    - The Called go in lists of a thousand. Ch 11: "Agathe Hask was not in the first thousand. She was in the third." Ch 17: "Three thousand people were in it by now", "seven thousand more coming", and "In the first night forty people died".
    - Ch 20: "four thousand of them"; then "Four thousand," she said. / "Nearer five. The fourth list came into the Cold yesterday. They came too." Later: "five thousand of the Called".
    - Why both can't be true: four lists of a thousand, less the dead, make at most about 4,000, not "nearer five". Nothing says anyone else joined.
    - Severity: **minor**

17. **Gallery Twenty-Two as the last gallery before the Lip**
    - Chapters: 2, 5, 17
    - Ch 5: "Twenty-Two was the last gallery before the Lip."
    - Ch 2: "Old Pask. Wim Pask, Twenty-Three." and "Gallery Twenty-Three had come for Wim Pask, and so had a good part of Twenty-Two and Twenty-Four". Ch 17: "All of Twenty-Two had come out, and Twenty-One and Twenty-Three".
    - Why both can't be true: gallery numbers rise toward the Lip ("below Fifteen" is the dim, poorer end). If 22 is the last before the Lip, then 23 and 24 would be in the uninhabited Cold. Yet they are lived-in galleries. This is weaker if numbering also runs round the circumference.
    - Severity: **trivial**

Counts: serious 1, minor 9, trivial 7 (total 17)
