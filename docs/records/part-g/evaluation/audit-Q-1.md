# Continuity audit: Book Q

1. **Basu "built" a Tally that is ninety years old**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 19 (also 21)
   - Ch 1: the Tally "has kept you safe for ninety years"; Ch 4 (Achterberg): "It was built, ninety years ago, to make sure no one on this rock ever took air that was not paid for." Ch 21: the Tally "had kept every account on the rock every hour on the hour for eight hundred years."
   - Ch 4: Basu, "a small, neat, brown man of fifty or so", says "The Tally can't be unlocked without stopping it. I built it so. I was twenty-six". Ch 19: "I made it the first year... When I built her. I was twenty-six" and "I've thought about that every night for twenty-six years."
   - Why both can't be true: by his own figures Basu built the Tally about 26 years before the Closing (around 2361). But everyone else, the narrator included, dates it to the colony's founding about 90 years earlier. Nothing in the book says it was rebuilt or replaced.
   - Severity: **serious**

2. **Fen says the names were cut "for three hundred years"**
   - Chapters: 6 (against 1, 3, 7, 13)
   - Ch 6 (Fen): "Somebody cut every one of these by hand, for three hundred years, so that the people who died on that rock wouldn't just be numbers".
   - Ch 3 (Fen herself): the *Ardent Mercy* crossed "in 2296", and the bell left at the Closing in 2387. Ch 7 (Odile): "Ninety-one years she's hung there."
   - Why both can't be true: the bell was on Lantern for 91 years before it left. Fen is the archivist who dates the hands, and she gives those dates herself in Ch 3. Three hundred years is roughly the length of the later Owing era, when the bell was gone.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **Marisol Tey's daughter: born 3086, but "six" in 3089**
   - Chapters: 12, 15
   - Ch 12 (pod card): "Dependants: one (1), female, b. 3086, retained Kessane".
   - Ch 15 (Marisol): "It attaches on majority. She's six."
   - Why both can't be true: the story is set in 3089, so a child born in 3086 is at most three. She would be younger still at whatever point her mother went into cold sleep.
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **Joss is a hundred metres along the Wick, at its middle collimator**
   - Chapter: 7
   - "There were forty collimators on the Wick, ring after ring of them down the eleven-kilometre length of the lamp"; "Joss had the middle."
   - "He was on the middle ring, a hundred metres along... he had his big arms wrapped round the housing of the twentieth collimator and his face turned toward her, and he was grinning".
   - Why both can't be true: forty collimators spread over 11 km put the twentieth about 5 km from the master ring, not 100 m. From the root Dagny could not see his face or his grin.
   - Severity: **minor**

5. **When the "closing phase" was said to begin**
   - Chapters: 1, 10
   - Ch 1 (announcement): "Three weeks ago the station recorded the beginning of what our physicists call a closing phase."
   - Ch 10 (Basu): "The notice came in on the data-run three weeks before the Director's announcement... Three weeks later, the throat-watch recorded the onset of a closing phase."
   - Why both can't be true: by Basu's account the closing phase was claimed three weeks after the notice, which is the day of the announcement. The announcement itself dates the onset three weeks earlier, on the day the notice arrived. Basu's whole point is the order of these events, and the two timelines do not match.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **What a child costs to keep**
   - Chapters: 1, 4
   - Ch 1: "the ordinary minus three hundred that a Lantern child had by eight from being fed and housed and breathed."
   - Ch 4 (Hesketh): "You've given us six hundred credits of rope, Director. That's a year for a child."
   - Why both can't be true: Ch 1 has a child's keep running about 37 credits a year, so 600 credits would cover about sixteen years, not one. (It could be argued that most of a child's costs go on a parent's account, but Ch 1 attributes the 300 directly to the child being fed, housed and breathing.)
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **Dagny's balance after the strike**
   - Chapters: 7, 13
   - Ch 7: "four hundred and eleven doors on Lantern had dimmed... Dagny's was one of them"; her door "would stay dimmed until she found five hundred credits she did not have". Ch 13 still has her sleeping in the locker room "since her door on Gallery Six had dimmed".
   - Ch 13: "I'm in credit. Just. I've got six hundred and eleven." The transfer is then refused, yet her ration still comes "because she was still, by eleven credits, on the right side of the line".
   - Why both can't be true: inside Ch 13 her margin is 611 and then 11, with no transfer between the two. Eleven is the figure she would have had if the refused transfer of 600 had gone through. Both figures also clash with a door that is still dimmed for lack of credit.
   - Severity: **minor**

8. **How long four cassettes last**
   - Chapters: 14 (and 11)
   - Ch 14 (Ninian): "Each one burns about a third of her length for three years, or the whole of her for one. Fourteen... For fourteen years." Ch 11 says the same: at two-thirds length, "a little under a year and a half"; at full length, "a little under one."
   - Ch 14, moments later (Tamsin): "Four's a year at full length... Barely three at a third."
   - Why both can't be true: by the stated rule, four cassettes last four years at full length and about twelve at a third. Tamsin's figures are wrong both ways: a shorter lamp would make the fuel run out sooner. Nothing in the text flags it as a mistake.
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **How many people were Called**
   - Chapters: 11, 17, 20
   - Ch 11/17: each list is "a thousand", and "Three thousand people were in it by now", with forty dead on the first night and more after.
   - Ch 20: "Nearer five. The fourth list came into the Cold yesterday"; "five thousand of the Called"; Vosk: "five thousand people".
   - Why both can't be true: four lists of about a thousand people each, minus the dead, is fewer than 4,000. "Nearer five" thousand would need a fifth list, or extra people the book never mentions.
   - Severity: **minor**

10. **The Cold "uninhabited beyond memory" while the lamp kept shrinking**
    - Chapters: 2, 14
    - Ch 2: "nobody had lived in the Cold Wards for longer than anybody had been alive"; the Lip post is "an iron spike hammered into the road by some cutter a century dead".
    - Ch 14 (Ninian, master for forty years): "I've shortened her six times since then... watched another two hundred metres of her go dark, and another gallery go cold."
    - Why both can't be true: if about 1.2 km of lamp went dark and six galleries went cold within the last forty years, living people once lived in what is now the Cold. The point where the light ends, the Lip, would also have moved well past a post set a century ago.
    - Severity: **minor**

11. **Spine winches "rusted solid for a century", though Ninian loads by winch**
    - Chapters: 14, 20
    - Ch 14 (Ninian): "I've loaded every cassette that's gone into her for fifty years... You move it on a sled, on rails, with a winch."
    - Ch 20: "The cargo winches on the spine had rusted solid in the century since anyone had hauled anything up them."
    - Why both can't be true: a cassette lasts about one to one and a half years. So for fifty years one has been moved up to the master-ring port every year or so, by winch. Nobody can have gone a century without hauling anything up.
    - Severity: **minor**

12. **"Five words" that are six**
    - Chapter: 8
    - "there were five words. / SHE SAID SHE WOULD COME BACK."
    - Why both can't be true: the line has six words.
    - Severity: **trivial**

13. **How long Captain Ode has run throats**
    - Chapters: 3, 12, 18
    - Ch 3 (Ode): "for the first time in thirty years of running throats".
    - Ch 12 (Ode): "I've run throats for thirty-four years"; Ch 18 (dying): "Thirty-four years".
    - Why both can't be true: she gives two different figures for the same career within a few weeks.
    - Severity: **trivial**

14. **How many bailiffs Kell has**
    - Chapters: 3, 18
    - Ch 3: "Ten men and women were coming up the dock... At their head was a heavy, unhurried man of fifty with a sergeant's flash".
    - Ch 18 (Haste): "Sergeant Kell's people are very good, but there are twelve of them."
    - Why both can't be true: none join or leave during the voyage. The count is ten, or eleven with Kell, but never twelve.
    - Severity: **trivial**

15. **Dacey's days of listening**
    - Chapter: 9
    - "The signal came on the third day out from the Throat".
    - "he had been listening to the silence of the Sabra system for four days".
    - Why both can't be true: he began listening as the ship came out of the Throat (Ch 6), so by the third day he cannot have been listening for four.
    - Severity: **trivial**

16. **The bell came down "two days ago"**
    - Chapters: 16, 19
    - Ch 16: the last name is cut "at eleven at night on the forty-seventh day"; "They took the bell down at two in the morning" (that is, early on day 48), before the root at 7:05 on day 48.
    - Ch 19, after 4 a.m. on the forty-ninth day: "She had seen it last at two in the morning, two days ago".
    - Why both can't be true: only about 27 hours have passed, so it was the previous night, not two days ago.
    - Severity: **trivial**

17. **The date of the frame-40 break-in**
    - Chapters: 14, 17, 20
    - Ch 14 (Abney): "The third list goes on the twenty-fourth." Ch 17: "They went in on the night before the third list", and Agathe leaves with the third list at second shift that same day.
    - Ch 17: "at two in the morning on the twenty-third day". Ch 20, on the twenty-seventh day: "he had opened its twin in the floor of the fifth hall three nights before".
    - Why both can't be true: if the third list leaves on the 24th, the night before ends on the 24th, and "three nights before" the 27th also points to the 24th. "The twenty-third day" is one day off.
    - Severity: **trivial**

18. **How old the keel-ways are**
    - Chapters: 3/19, 20
    - The colony opened in 2296 (Ch 3); the docks under the Counting House were cut "ninety years before" 2387 (Ch 19). The Owing era (311 AC) is therefore about 2698.
    - Ch 20: the keel-ways were cut by "the Company's engineers... three and a half centuries ago".
    - Why both can't be true: the Company built the rock about 400 years before 311 AC, not 350.
    - Severity: **trivial**

19. **Dagny's "long weeks" in the infirmary**
    - Chapter: 19
    - "Varga told her, in the long weeks after, in the infirmary, when he came to sit with her".
    - The same chapter: the *Covenant* comes home "eleven days later" (after day 52), and Dagny dies at the Hook, where she had cut the shackle "seventeen days before" (day 48). That puts her death around day 65.
    - Why both can't be true: she had about thirteen days after the backwash and about two days back on Lantern. That is not "long weeks".
    - Severity: **trivial**

20. **The timetable for the approach to Lantern**
    - Chapters: 18, 21
    - Ch 18, at night after the morning meeting: "in eighteen hours the *Good Title* would clear the limb". Ch 21: Corvin has been reading "since about midnight", and the ship clears the limb soon after 6 a.m. Haste then says "We'll be docking within two hours."
    - Ch 21: the ship "came in through the open inner lock of the Dock Ring at noon by the ship's clock". After the speeches, the crowd and the trip down to the root, the reading still takes "three hours and nine minutes" and ends "at nine minutes past three in the afternoon". By that arithmetic it began at exactly noon, the moment of docking.
    - Why both can't be true: the limb is cleared about six hours after midnight, not eighteen. And the naming cannot start at the same minute the ship enters the lock. (It is possible the two clocks differ, but the book never says so.)
    - Severity: **trivial**

**Counts by severity: 1 serious, 10 minor, 9 trivial (20 total).**
