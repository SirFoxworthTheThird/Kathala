# Continuity audit: book-S (21 chapters, read in full)

1. **The First Keeping has nine pilots but only eight people**
   - Chapters: 16, 19
   - Ch 16 (Vane): "I have eight pilots. I need nine." Ch 19: "there were nine pilots standing in front of them in a line". The roll that follows: "Haldane… The Moss brothers, both of them… Ida Okonjo… Jory Ferris… Big Sven… Marguerite… And Corrigan's empty place, which nobody stood in, and then Teo." Then: "Nine. Eight debts, and Nico." Salome's blessing: "*Haldane.* *Tomas Moss.* *Ewan Moss.* *Ida.* *Jory.* *Sven.* *Marguerite.* She came to Teo last." Later: "She had called the line for nine tugs into the throat and eight out of it".
   - Why both can't be true: The named pilots are seven debt pilots plus Teo, which makes eight. Corrigan's place is explicitly empty. "Eight debts, and Nico", nine tugs, and eight returning tugs all need eight debt pilots besides Teo. One pilot is missing from the line and from the blessing.
   - Severity: **serious**

2. **The Thirtieth Keeping's clock: "in four hours" on 5 December, yet the door opens on the 6th, "two hours after" the Keepers went in**
   - Chapters: 20 (with 17)
   - Ch 20: "They came out of the shear at three in the morning on the fifth of December". Greaves says: "The Keeping flies in four hours." The reading and tuning take "twenty-two minutes", and then "the readers are calling the hour… They're calling the Keepers in." Later: "The door opened at thirty-one minutes past seven on the morning of the sixth of December… two hours after the last three Keepers went in."
   - Why both can't be true: The plan is to hold for about thirty minutes, and the skiffs go in right after the reading. That puts their launch around 07:00 on 5 December. If the door opened two hours after they went in, it opened on the morning of the 5th, not at 07:31 on the 6th, about a day later. Ch 17 ("on the sixth of December, when the door has opened") suggests the Keeping was meant for the night of 5–6 December. If so, the 3 a.m. "fifth" arrival plus "four hours" is wrong.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **Nine skiffs launch, but only seven are accounted for**
   - Chapter: 20
   - "a line of small lights. Nine." Then: "the third light turned round" (Pell's). "a fourth light turned. Abbe Sorrell. Then a fifth. Then a sixth." Then: "Three did not." Finally: "The sixth skiff came in alongside the *Vigil*, and then the fifth and the fourth, and last of all the third".
   - Why both can't be true: Read by position, as the homecoming order shows, skiffs 3, 4, 5 and 6 turned back and three went on. That makes seven. Skiffs 1 and 2, which launched first ("The first. The second. Pell's, the third"), are never turned or sent in. The later line "One of them came out. The other two did not." also counts only three going in. (If "fourth/fifth/sixth" means the order of turning, the arrival sentence contradicts that reading.)
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **Cassius Mor's time as High Warden: nineteen years or twenty-five**
   - Chapters: 5, 17, 20
   - Ch 5: "He had declined every one for nineteen years." Same chapter: "He has declined every petition for substitution since he was invested. Two hundred and six of them, at the last three Draws." Ch 17: "when he read it, at forty-one… And then he was High Warden for twenty-five years." (He is 66 in ch 2.) Ch 20 (Mor): "I've believed that for twenty-five years."
   - Why both can't be true: Twenty-five years agrees with his age and with "three Draws" (the 28th, 29th and 30th). Nineteen years would cover only two Draws.
   - Severity: **minor**

5. **The sleeve names' motives are dated after the Draws they explain**
   - Chapters: 8 (with 2, 5, 14)
   - Ch 8: "*Twenty-Sixth: Orla Venning, daughter of Assembly Member Tomas Venning (… voted against the Wardens' appropriation, 2573).*" Also: "*Twenty-Seventh: Hal Brisk, son of the Shoals delegate Ana Brisk (moved the motion for Shoals quota relief, 2584).*"
   - Why both can't be true: The cycle is every 4,000 days, about 10.95 years. The 30th is in 2608, and ch 5 puts the 29th "eleven years ago" (about 2597). That places the 28th around 2586, the 27th around 2575, and the 26th around 2564. A 2573 vote cannot be the reason for a 2564 Draw, and a 2584 motion cannot be the reason for a 2575 Draw. Both dates are one cycle late. The 28th and 29th entries (the 27th Keeping, 2596) are correct.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **The Lamplighter "five years" at the Lantern House, but she sang her son off from its steps eleven years ago**
   - Chapters: 3, 6
   - Ch 3: "Mercy Oduya had been preaching from the old Assembly steps in Tallow for twenty-five years and from the Lantern House for five". Ch 6 (Mercy): "He went in at the Fifty-Ninth… and I sang him off from the Lantern House steps… for eleven years". Pike (ch 6): "They'd been in the Lantern House for weeks, singing".
   - Why both can't be true: The Lantern House was her base at the 59th, eleven years ago. It cannot have been her pulpit for only five.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **"Nobody alive had seen" a Keeper's skiff flown, yet living people flew them at the 58th and 59th**
   - Chapters: 3, 6, 18
   - Ch 3: "A Keeper's skiff, the narrow kind… the kind they had flown in the Keepings before the Quiet, Hanne said, though nobody alive had seen one flown. Twelve metres nose to tail." In the same chapter Hanne says of Pike: "He flew escort for her, at the Fifty-Ninth." Ch 6 (Pike): "We took the Keepers out to the mouth. Seven skiffs." Ch 18: Tobin's craft from the 59th is "A skiff. Small. Twelve metres. A Keeper's skiff."
   - Why both can't be true: Eleven years earlier, the same twelve-metre Keeper's skiffs were flown in public: seven at the 59th, nine at the 58th. Pike, Rusk, Mercy and Hanne's own neighbours saw it. Hanne knows Pike was there, so this isn't a believable mistake on her part.
   - Severity: **minor**

8. **The strata are described in the wrong order**
   - Chapter: 15
   - First description: "two of them, very thick… and in the middle, a long run of medium bands, steady… thirty or so of them; and then, below those, nearer the bottom, a stretch where the bands thinned out again and became irregular and faint". The check against the Book of Keepings: "*Fifty-Ninth: seven skiffs.* … *Fifty-Eighth: nine.* … *Fifty-Seventh: two. Fifty-Sixth: one. Fifty-Fifth: none flown.*" are thin bands directly under the two thick ones. Then: "Below the Forty-Second… the long run of medium bands went on down, steady, thirty-odd of them".
   - Why both can't be true: The check puts the thin, irregular stretch (the decline before the Lantern) directly under the two thick top bands, with the steady medium run (the Compact) below it near the bottom. The first description has it the other way round: the medium run in the middle and the thin stretch below it.
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **Juno's age at her commissioning**
   - Chapters: 2, 5
   - Ch 5 (Adaeze): "I saw you, once, at your commissioning. You were nineteen." Ch 2 (Pell): "you remember when we went up to the Anchorhold for your commissioning… I was eight." Also in ch 2: "when she was six and Juno was eighteen", which makes the sisters twelve years apart (31 and 19). Ch 5: "At the Twenty-Ninth Draw, eleven years ago, when Juno was twenty… stayed quiet through their last year, and two days after commissioning".
   - Why both can't be true: If Pell was eight, Juno was twenty. The Rhee timeline places the commissioning after the 29th Draw, when Juno was already twenty. She can't have been nineteen.
   - Severity: **trivial**

10. **"Six weeks parked at the Anchorhold" from an order starting 20 November**
    - Chapters: 5, 8, 11
    - Ch 5: "with station at the Anchorhold from the twentieth of November until the Narrowing." Marsh, reading the order: "Six weeks parked at the Anchorhold". The Narrowing is 5 December (ch 8: "on the twenty-eighth of October, with thirty-eight days to go"; ch 11: "going in on the fifth of December").
    - Why both can't be true: 20 November to 5 December is about fifteen days, not six weeks.
    - Severity: **trivial**

11. **The swell log began on 1 March, a day before the Narrowing was observed**
    - Chapters: 1, 4, 13
    - Ch 13: "the little column in his notebook that had begun on the night of the first of March with the word *probably*." Ch 1: the observation, the reading of 61%, the broadcast, and then "At two in the morning… He wrote a note in the log — *low-frequency swell…probably instrumental*", all on the day of "the night of the second of March". Ch 4 (Vane): "the waist at sixty-one per cent of nominal on the second".
    - Why both can't be true: The *probably* note was written on the night of the 2nd, not the 1st.
    - Severity: **trivial**

12. **"Eighteen days" to the trough on both 11 and 13 April**
    - Chapter: 13
    - On the 11th: "The waist would go on narrowing for another eighteen days". On the 13th, Ambrose: "will go on narrowing for eighteen days, which it will", with the trough "on the first of May".
    - Why both can't be true: 13 April to 1 May is eighteen days. From the 11th it is twenty. Both statements can't be right.
    - Severity: **trivial**

13. **The speed-up arithmetic for the recording**
    - Chapters: 13, 21
    - "Then he sped it up. A thousand times. So that a month of the long tide went by in forty minutes". The same recording is introduced as "Forty days of it, in forty minutes" (ch 13 canteen, and ch 21 playback).
    - Why both can't be true: At 1000×, forty minutes holds about 28 days, roughly a month. Forty days would take about 58 minutes. "A month" and "forty days" can't both be forty minutes.
    - Severity: **trivial**

14. **The Fork is struck at 4 a.m. as the Keepers "went into the mouth", but at 4 a.m. they were still standing on the dock**
    - Chapter: 19
    - "There were nine tugs on Dock Four at four in the morning on the first of May… nine pilots standing in front of them in a line". Then come the blessing, the Director's speech, and the flight through the shear. Later: the fork "had been struck at four in the morning on the first of May… at the moment the Keepers went into the mouth".
    - Why both can't be true: The Keepers couldn't enter the mouth at the hour they were lined up on the dock. The trip through the lanes and the shear takes a long time.
    - Severity: **trivial**

15. **Shaft Nine's headcount**
    - Chapters: 7, 10, 13
    - Ch 7: "The canteen was full, three shifts' worth, a hundred and forty cutters and loaders and cage-men". Ch 10: "It was all of them, all three shifts, two hundred people". Ch 13: "two hundred of them".
    - Why both can't be true: All three shifts are given as 140 in one place and 200 in another.
    - Severity: **trivial**

16. **The Tally's last lines: where the second words sit, and "FOR CLARE"**
    - Chapters: 10, 16, 20
    - Ch 10: Lulu "scratched underneath it, smaller… PILOT". Ch 16: Teo "under it, smaller, she scratched: HARP." Ch 20: "there were four lines… they had a second word after each name", shown as "C. DACE — PILOT", "A. LYLE — HARP", "T. KESH — PILOT", "FOR CLARE".
    - Why both can't be true: PILOT and HARP were scratched on separate lines below the names, not on the same line after them. "FOR CLARE" is not a name followed by a second word.
    - Severity: **trivial**

Counts: serious 1, minor 7, trivial 8 (16 total).
