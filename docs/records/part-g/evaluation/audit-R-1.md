# Continuity audit: book R

1. **How long Pell has been in the Nursery: eleven years or six?**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 13, 19
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 1: "eleven pictures that Pell had drawn and sent down on the visitation shuttle, one a year since she was taken"
     - Ch. 1: "Maren had been up here a hundred and thirty-two times" (monthly visitation, so 11 years)
     - Ch. 4: "under Pell's eleven drawings"
     - On the other side, Ch. 1: "the grey Nursery smock that had swamped her at six"; "since the first visitation, when Pell was six"; "I said I'm twelve."
     - Ch. 4: "At six, the day before they took her"
     - Ch. 13: "Maren had not touched her daughter in six years"
     - Ch. 19: "the way she had held it up to the Nursery glass once a month for six years"
   - Why both can't be true: Pell was taken at six and is now twelve. That is six years, so about six drawings and about 72 visits. Eleven yearly drawings and 132 monthly visits mean eleven years in the Nursery, which would put her taking at age one. Maren's own age (38) and her bond history (surety triggered at 32) also fit six years.
   - Severity: **serious**

2. **Anselm's gutter balance is still above zero in the current volumes, but his sheet says it hit zero in Year 280**
   - Chapters: 2 (and 17)
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 2: the pencilled figure in volumes 204–206 (206 is the volume in use now) is "a sum that began somewhere and was being steadily reduced… This was a number in the low millions… The same running figure, growing. Not growing. Shrinking."
     - Ch. 2: the leaf is "the same work she had just been tracing through the gutters of three books, but complete… *Zero. Year 280.*"
     - Ch. 17: the sheet has a line per year with "the box drawn round Year 280"
   - Why both can't be true: if the running balance reached zero in Year 280, the gutter totals in the volumes for the last few years (around 335–341) can't be a positive figure in the low millions that is still being paid down and still charged Founding Terms. The text calls the two "the same work", so they can't be different calculations.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **How old the book was when the Sorrow pages were forged**
   - Chapter: 5
   - Quotes:
     - "Volume Forty-one, which covered the hundred and thirty-eighth to the hundred and forty-second years"
     - The forged pages were "sewn into a book that was a hundred and twenty years old at the time they were made"
     - Rebinding dated "*The 279th year of the Count*", by "somebody… in the two hundred and seventy-ninth year"
   - Why both can't be true: a volume written around year 140 was about 139 years old in year 279, not 120.
   - Severity: **trivial**

4. **Union rescue: "ten days" between the Factor's speech and Farrant's**
   - Chapters: 7, 10
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 7: "The Factor came down to the Sump on the tenth day"
     - Ch. 10: "By the morning of the nineteenth day… He stood on the separator platform where the Factor had stood ten days before"
   - Why both can't be true: day 10 to day 19 is nine days.
   - Severity: **trivial**

5. **The herald has spoken "for four days" and Wren has learned letters "for three days" when less than two days have passed**
   - Chapters: 3, 6, 9
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 3: the herald first speaks in Rook's cabin on the afternoon he finds Wren ("The humming stopped… a voice began to speak").
     - Ch. 6: that night Haddie translates it. The Council meets "at the eighth hour" the next morning. They flee and hide "behind the moonlet for six hours", then after the "fourteenth hour" broadcast turn "toward Kell".
     - Ch. 9: "the whole of the six-hour run from the rings"; "We'd come in at the half past three"; then, at the Hulk, "I measured it at the seventh hour, on the way in from Kell. And again just now."
     - Ch. 9 claims: "It had been speaking its notice every hour or so… for four days" and "You've been learning them for three days."
   - Why both can't be true: by the stated hours the herald has been speaking for under two days. Wren began learning on the moonlet, about a day earlier. Nothing in the text adds the missing days.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **Corley is counted among the fourteen, but he has to be told about the strike**
   - Chapters: 7, 10, 13
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 7: at the vote on the water "the hands went up… until they all were"; the strike date is to be kept secret until "the seventeenth night".
     - Ch. 10: at the rack gate on strike morning, Maren tells each breather "*We're not diving*… Some had known already. Some hadn't." Corley pleads "*I've got a ward too, Tull, she's on the list, I can't, they'll put it on the bond*".
     - Ch. 13: Maren counts the fourteen on the platform: "Hatch. Ruth. Ade. Pym. Farrant… Moss. The Ellerys, two. Corley. Brand…"
   - Why both can't be true: someone who had voted for the strike on the platform would not be learning about it, and arguing, at the gate. It can be explained only if he lost his nerve, and the scene is written as him being told.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **Thirty-four people in the hall, when the count gives thirty-five**
   - Chapters: 9, 12
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 9: "When we were all at the top, all thirty-two of them"; "Thirty-two children, a girl of nineteen, an old woman of seventy-four and me"
     - Ch. 12: "thirty-four people breathing in a room built for none"
   - Why both can't be true: 32 + Wren + Haddie + Rook = 35. Mattie is dead and isn't breathing.
   - Severity: **trivial**

8. **Rook's timeline: Jory's death and Rook quitting**
   - Chapters: 3, 6, 15, 18
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 3: Wren's tithe was "the twenty-first year", and she has had "Ten years in the vents" (so year 21 was ten years ago).
     - Ch. 6: "since I took off the reeve's coat seven years ago"; "a reeve for nine years"
     - Ch. 15: Kask, about year 21: "He'd been my reeve for six years"
     - Ch. 15: "He died two years later. Red fever… I went back to Kell the next week and told the Margrave I was done." ("Two years after" again in Ch. 18.)
   - Why both can't be true: Jory dying two years after the year-21 tithe, and Rook quitting a week later, puts the quitting eight years ago, not seven. "Six years" by year 21 plus a nine-year career puts it three years after the tithe, which also doesn't fit "two years later… the next week".
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **Castellane says the Grace "passes Gallows tomorrow"**
   - Chapters: 10, 13, 16, 19
   - Quotes:
     - Ch. 10: "The *Grace* at convoy speed, with escort… four days"
     - Ch. 13: "The convoy will depart as scheduled, at first bell tomorrow"
     - Ch. 16, said the night before departure: "When the *Grace* passes Gallows tomorrow, I was going to set the pins to invert behind her."
     - Ch. 19: more than three days later the Grace is still "eighteen hours behind us"
   - Why both can't be true: the Grace leaves the next morning and needs about four days to reach Gallows. She can't pass it "tomorrow".
   - Severity: **minor**

10. **Corin's "twenty years" of Nullist circles started the night of Tamsin's death, which was fifteen years ago**
    - Chapters: 14, 17
    - Quotes:
      - Ch. 14: "I've been doing this twenty years, Aurek"; "I've been scratching circles for twenty years"
      - Ch. 17: "I scratched my first circle that night… Twenty years." and "Tamsin went down in the three hundred and twenty-sixth year. … Forty-six years after it was paid."
      - The current year is "the three hundred and forty-first year"
    - Why both can't be true: 341 − 326 = 15 years, not 20. Twenty years would also make Corin (about 45) a father at about 11.
    - Severity: **minor**

11. **"Three nights ago" in the Reliquary**
    - Chapters: 11, 14, 17, 20
    - Quotes:
      - Ch. 20: the tag she had seen "three nights ago in the dark of the Reliquary hanging from the arm of a plain metal chair"
      - After the Reliquary (Ch. 11) come: the "second night" at Corin's, Piet taken "on the third morning", the audit "the next day", the chapel that night, Piet freed the next morning, then departure (Ch. 14). Then the voyage: "second day out… six days… the seventh night… On the eighth day they saw it… We'll know in a day" (Ch. 17).
    - Why both can't be true: the Reliquary visit was at least thirteen days earlier, not three nights.
    - Severity: **minor**

12. **The oldest volumes of the Ninth are said to charge "pin operations" from before the Starving**
    - Chapters: 2, 8, 20
    - Quotes:
      - Ch. 2: the Ninth's volumes go "back in grey and then brown and then black bindings to the first decades after the Starving"
      - Ch. 8: "When the Door was shut… You know what happened next. The Starving."
      - Ch. 20: "*Pin.* That was in the oldest volumes of the Ninth… a charge, once, at the very beginning, before the Starving, for something called *pin operations*"; "the oldest volumes of the Ninth had charged *pin operations*… every month, at the very beginning"
    - Why both can't be true: the pins ran only while the Door was open, before the Starving. The Ninth's oldest volumes date from after it. (Haddie's separate Combine-era book in Ch. 12 can hold such charges. The Ninth can't.)
    - Severity: **minor**

13. **Gallows is put "three hundred and forty years" back, when the dates give 363**
    - Chapters: 8, 17, 20, plus the era headers
    - Quotes:
      - Headers: "*The Bond · 2431*" and "*The Ledger · 2794*"
      - Ch. 17: the outer door "rimed with three hundred and forty years of frost"; "nothing to show that anyone had been there in three hundred and forty years"
      - Ch. 20: "A diver from the Lungs, three hundred and forty years ago"; "Three hundred and forty years in the cold"
      - Ch. 8: the First Auditor "was young when the Door was shut — very young… when she was about your age… she wrote the first Count"
    - Why both can't be true: the Count is in its 341st year, but it began only once the First Auditor was grown, and Pell was 12 in 2431. The closing at Gallows was 2794 − 2431 = 363 years earlier. The narration, and the book's own legend of the First Auditor, are at odds with the "340 years" figure. This is close third person and could be read as Ysolde's assumption, but the narration states it as fact.
    - Severity: **minor**

14. **The recording Quist hears doesn't match the recording Maren made**
    - Chapters: 19, 20
    - Quotes:
      - Ch. 19: the line comes near the start: "I don't know if anyone will ever come here again. I hope they do. I hope it's you." Near the end: "Whoever hears this… If it isn't her. If it's somebody else, a long time after." No "Pell" follows.
      - Ch. 20: "when the voice said *whoever hears this, if it isn't her*, and paused, and said again, more quietly, *I hope it's you, Pell*, the Prelate-Auditor's face went white"
    - Why both can't be true: the line that sets off Quist's recognition isn't in the recording as Chapter 19 gives it. "I hope it's you" comes at the beginning, without "Pell", and the "Whoever hears this" passage is addressed to someone other than Pell.
    - Severity: **minor**

15. **Ysolde knows Maren's first name**
    - Chapters: 11, 19, 20, 21
    - Quotes:
      - Ch. 20: all Ysolde finds on the body is "TULL — H-11873", and she tells Quist "Her name's on her suit."
      - Ch. 21, her letter: "The woman in the chair is Maren Tull."
    - Why both can't be true: nothing Ysolde sees or hears gives her "Maren".
      - The recording never says the name: she says "It's Mum", and the band voices say only "Arkwright", "the bond", "she".
      - The First Book entries she glimpsed list "a number, a surname, an initial".
      - Quist knows only "Pell".
      - The text never shows her finding the name anywhere else.
    - Severity: **minor**
