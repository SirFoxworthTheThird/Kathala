# Continuity audit: book P

All 21 chapters read in full. Each item below was checked twice against the text. Quotes are verbatim.

1. **Hesketh is dead before he can have taught Maren, or made her chief**
   - Chapters: 1, 7, 16, 19
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 1: "Maren had learned the voice from her own chief, Hesketh, who had gone into Gall the year Abel was born"
     - Ch 7 (Odile): "You did four years at twenty kilometres. Before Hesketh made you chief."
     - Ch 16: "the way Hesketh had taught her twenty-six years ago"
     - Ch 16, the coat: "TSOLO stencilled in white across the back where it had said HESKETH for twenty years"
     - Ch 19: "the way Hesketh had taught her to write an orbit down when she was a runner of twenty"
   - Why it can't be true: Abel is 29 (Ch 4, Ch 13: "twenty-nine years old"), so Ch 1 puts Hesketh's death 29 years ago, when Maren was 12. Maren has been chief for nine years (Ch 1), so Hesketh cannot have made her chief. Hesketh also cannot have taught her 26 years ago, or when she was 20 (21 years ago). The other passages agree with each other: Hesketh was chief from about 29 years ago until about 9 years ago, which is the coat's "twenty years". Only Ch 1's date of death disagrees.
   - Severity: **minor**

2. **Maren's years on the line don't fit her age**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 7, 10, 16
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 1: "Maren had been tether-chief for nine years and on the line for twenty-six before that"
     - Ch 7 and Ch 10: "some old animal thing that had lived through twenty-six years on the line" / "the animal that lived through twenty-six years on the line"
     - Ch 16: "the way Hesketh had taught her twenty-six years ago"
   - Why it can't be true: Maren is 41. Ekene is "fifty-two, eleven years older than she was" (Ch 1), and Abel, 29, was 3 when she was 15. Nine years plus twenty-six "before that" makes 35 years on the line, so she would have started at 6. The later chapters treat 26 years as her whole time on the line, starting at 15, the year her mother died. Ch 1's "before that" breaks this.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **The Ocampo "household of five" has six people**
   - Chapters: 2, 5, 14, 20
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 2: "Household of five." Pilar then says: "Mama's at the plant. Papa's at the plant. Abuela's asleep. Tomás and Juli are asleep."
     - Ch 5, with the grandmother still alive that cycle: "two hundred litres an hour for five people"
     - Ch 14: "Pilar's mother is there too, and her father, and the two little ones"
   - Why it can't be true: Mother, father, Abuela, Tomás, Juli and Pilar make six. Ch 14 confirms that Tomás and Juli are Pilar's younger siblings. "Household of five" only becomes correct in Ch 20, after Abuela has died.
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **The drum hall is "eleven metres" long**
   - Chapters: 1, 7 (and 2)
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 7 (Odile): "Eleven metres is the length of the drum hall."
     - Ch 1: "the drum, the size of a cathedral"
     - Ch 1: "the drum hall, which was the only room on the *Patience* big enough to hold the whole crew"
     - Ch 2: the drum is "as long as a street"
   - Why it can't be true: An 11-metre room cannot hold a cathedral-sized drum and 300 crew. In Ch 13 it also holds "twice that many from the Camp".
   - Severity: **minor**

5. **How many lines are stencilled on the egg: three or four?**
   - Chapters: 7, 11, 17, 20, 21
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 7: "stencilled in black, in neat Combine capitals, were three lines" (SANITATION AGENT / PATTERN H / FACTOR'S AUTHORITY ONLY)
     - Ch 17, Isaura looking at it herself: "stencilled in black, in neat old capitals, three lines."
     - Ch 11 (Mattias, about the card): "a grey egg. In a cryostat. With those four lines stencilled on it", meaning the card's lines, "Asset Seven. Halloran-Vey Combine. ..."
     - Ch 20, Isaura's broadcast after seeing the egg: "It's got four lines stencilled on it and a green wheel."
   - Why it can't be true: The real stencil has three lines, and they are not the card's text. Isaura saw three lines in Ch 17 and says four in Ch 20.
   - Severity: **minor**

6. **Dasha has lived on the Ninth North for only twelve years, yet grew up there**
   - Chapters: 5, 14
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 5: "Twelve years I've lived on the Ninth North and I never once saw a warden give anybody air."
     - Ch 14: "My gran told it to me," says Dasha ... "When I was little. On the Ninth North."
   - Why it can't be true: She is "forty-odd" (Ch 5), so a childhood on the Ninth North means far more than twelve years there. The text gives no sign that she left and came back.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **Abel's drilling: "six hours" fits into four**
   - Chapter: 16
   - Quotes:
     - "He landed at the ninth hour"
     - "He drilled. It took him six hours. ... At the tenth hour ... At the twelfth ... At the thirteenth the auger stopped."
   - Why it can't be true: Landing at the ninth hour and finishing at the thirteenth leaves at most four hours of drilling.
   - Severity: **minor**

8. **How far the Lamp's closest pass is, on the day they find Abel**
   - Chapters: 11, 17, 20
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 11 (Corin): "The Lamp makes its closest pass to Gall on the ninth of August"
     - Ch 17, on the surface, on the 8th: "We're three days from its closest pass."
     - Ch 17, later the same day: "Gall turning in the sky, two days from its closest pass"
     - Ch 20, still the same day: "one day from the Lamp's closest pass"
   - Why it can't be true: The day is fixed as 8 August by "We go out at the start of the eighth" and by Ch 20's broadcast on the ninth, which mentions "Corin Strand shot at it yesterday". So the closest pass is one day away. Ch 17 gives three days and then two days within a few hours of each other.
   - Severity: **minor**

9. **Where the *Wick* landed: 50 m or 200 m from the tug**
   - Chapters: 17, 20
   - Quotes:
     - Ch 17: "The tug is fifty metres from where Corin set the *Wick* down."
     - Ch 17: the line runs "to the *Wick*, thirty metres off"
     - Ch 20, broadcast: "in a dipper skiff, under a black ridge, two hundred metres from a tug"
     - Ch 20: "where the *Wick* was sitting a minute ago, two hundred metres from the ridge"
   - Why it can't be true: The skiff never moves between these scenes, and the tug sits at the foot of the ridge. (Two hundred metres is the *Dray*'s distance in 2917, Ch 18.)
   - Severity: **minor**

10. **Ama Venn's "nine days" on the Rime**
    - Chapters: 14, 20
    - Quotes:
      - Ch 14: relocation begins "on the thirty-first of July", and she is in South Lock that day, the eve of the launch.
      - Ch 20 (Malachy): "The sixth. Before you'd even reached the Lamp. ... Nine days without a stop."
    - Why it can't be true: From 31 July to 6 August is at most six or seven days.
    - Severity: **minor**

11. **"Five weeks" since the Throat**
    - Chapters: 6, 18, 21
    - Quotes:
      - Ch 6: "They come out of the Corva lane on the twelfth of April"
      - Ch 21, on 2 May: "For the first time in five weeks, since the Throat, the cockpit isn't lit by it"
      - Ch 21: "a small white cracked moon whose shadow they've been watching cross the face of the giant for five weeks"
    - Why it can't be true: From 12 April to 2 May is 20 days, about three weeks. This fits Szabo's "I've been on the *Hymn* for three weeks" on 29 April (Ch 15).
    - Severity: **minor**

12. **Siege calendar in Chapter 8 is off by one or two days**
    - Chapters: 5, 8
    - Quotes:
      - Ch 5: "On the eighteenth day of the siege"; the lander is shot down that evening.
      - Ch 8 (Mattias): "flying a lander over Kettle's south ice on the thirtieth of June"
      - Ch 8: "Nine days after South Lock" and "Now, eight days into July and twenty-eight days into the siege"
    - Why it can't be true: Day 18 is 30 June, which makes day 1 the 13th of June, the dawn after Lampfast. Then "nine days after South Lock" is 9 July, "eight days into July" is siege day 26, and siege day 28 is 10 July. The three markers give three different dates.
    - Severity: **trivial**

13. **The air floor per person doesn't add up**
    - Chapters: 2, 5, 11
    - Quotes:
      - Ch 2: "The floor is three hundred an hour for five. It's enough to sleep on. It's enough to live on."
      - Ch 5: "Two hundred for five." This is the new floor.
      - Ch 11, for one prisoner: "I turn it to the floor. Two hundred and twenty." and "Nobody can live on ninety."
    - Why it can't be true: A household of five is said to live on 40 to 60 litres an hour each. Yet 90 litres an hour for one man is called unsurvivable, and his "floor" is 220.
    - Severity: **trivial**

14. **Abel's height**
    - Chapters: 4, 13, 16
    - Quotes:
      - Ch 4: "the way he used to when he was sixteen and had just grown taller than her"
      - Ch 4 and Ch 13: "twenty-nine years old, the same height as her"
      - Ch 16: "They had always been the same height."
    - Why it can't be true: If he outgrew her at sixteen, an adult Abel cannot be her height, let alone "always" have been.
    - Severity: **trivial**

Counts by severity: serious 0, minor 11, trivial 3 (14 in total).
