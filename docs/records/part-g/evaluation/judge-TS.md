# Judgement: the first book (T) vs the second book (S)

I read both books in full, every chapter. I read the first book (T) first.

- **The first book (T):** 21 chapters, about 99,000 words. Its three eras are *The Crossing* (CR 611), *The Account* (CR 929) and *The Salvage* (CR 1317).
- **The second book (S):** 21 chapters, about 90,000 words. Its three eras are *The Concession* (2291), *The Severance* (2608) and *The Ebb* (2937).

The two books are built in very similar ways. Each rotates its eras strictly (1-2-3, 1-2-3…). Each carries an artefact across the centuries: in T, the purser's key 0447 and the Book; in S, Ambrose Lyle's tuning fork. Each has a future era living under a founding myth that the past era shows being made. And each ends with a dead person's voice, recorded centuries earlier, being heard in the last era. So the comparison comes down to execution.

---

## The first book (T)

### 1. Interlacing, three distinct eras and casts: **10/10**
- **Strict rotation.** The chapters run Crossing / Account / Salvage from Chapter 1 to Chapter 21 without a break.
- **Three distinct casts.** No character crosses an era:
  - Annick Vell, Dace, Benn, Szell, Quarry and Ruck in the Crossing.
  - Hollis Fen, Marsh, Ottilie, Thorne, Calla Dray and Wren in the Account.
  - Yara Tull, Pim, Mitya, Sabeth Orrin, Kade, Quell and Dunn in the Salvage.
- **The eras are distinguished by their narration, not just their date tags:**
  - The Crossing is close third person, past tense.
  - The Account is Hollis's first-person confession.
  - The Salvage is present tense.
- **The anchors recur subtly and do real work:**
  - The ship becomes the Hulk.
  - Ballast Tank Four becomes the "Fourth Cistern" and then "the Widow".
  - Key 0447, the Ash Case, the plaque of Ordinances, and the coil-gang song "Heave the iron".
  - A Haskell index card dated 929 floats past Yara in Chapter 6.
  - The empty slot on the shelf for VOYAGE III.

### 2. Synchronized pacing: **9/10**
- **Each era has its own countdown,** and they tighten in step:
  - The Crossing counts the days to the Fork (35, then 17).
  - The Account counts the days to the Burning (12, 10, 8, 6).
  - The Salvage counts the Hulk's remaining passes (seven, six, five… one).
- **Each "act" lands in all three eras at once:**
  - Chapters 7–9: a betrayal or trap is sprung.
  - Chapters 10–12: Marit spaced, Hollis framed, Mitya's betrayal exposed.
  - Chapters 13–15: the eve of the Fork, Hollis a fugitive, Mitya's death.
  - Chapters 16–18: the mutiny, the Burning Day riot, the empty vault.
  - Chapters 19–21: the Book goes down the sea, Fen in the cistern, Yara's broadcast.
- **The finale is one handoff.** The secret is planted in Chapter 19, found in Chapter 20 and broadcast in Chapter 21.
- **One small unevenness:** the Crossing's action climax comes early (the mutiny at the Fork, Chapter 16). Its decisive act waits until Chapter 19.

### 3. No spoilers: **7/10**
The book's main device is the future's *false* memory of the past. The catechism (Chapter 2), Quell's sermon and the skipping song (Chapter 6), and the Ash Case all mislead deliberately, and the ending overturns them. The book's real secrets stay sealed until their own era reaches them:
- Annick discharges every debt under Article 20.
- She drops the true Book into Tank Four and burns VOYAGE III in its place.
- Szell murdered Benn.
- Quarry invented "Liberty".
- Bastian and Marsh killed Ottilie.

The *shape* of earlier endings still leaks early. Cases:

- **Chapter 2 (Account) on the Crossing.** The catechism tells us all of the following before the Crossing reaches the Fork (Chapter 16) or Landfall (Chapter 19):
  - the *Increase* reaches Absalom;
  - the bonded are not sold to Shale;
  - "the Founders saved us… they burned the Book";
  - a Liberty debt follows;
  - Annick is remembered as "the Purser [who] would have sold us".

  The mutiny's success and the public burning are therefore known in advance. The truth behind them is not.
- **Chapters 5 and 11 (Account) on the Crossing.** The vault shelf holds only VOYAGE I, II and IV ("Where's Three?" "Lost"). Thorne also shows that the founding page was grown nine years after Landfall under a council seal, with no purser's voice. Both are fair-play clues to Annick's substitution, arriving well before Chapter 19.
- **Chapters 3, 6 and 9 (Salvage) on the Account.**
  - There will be a "Night of the Hulk".
  - Hollis will be remembered as "Fen the Turncoat" who "stole something" and "ran off with the Concordance".
  - The Houses will be "gone" after the Dimming.
  - "Nobody on Tamar has paid a mark on the Account since the Night of the Hulk."

  Together these give away the broad outcome of Burning Day (the Sale fails, the Houses fall) eleven to fourteen chapters before Chapter 17. The legend is false and is explained in Chapter 14, which softens it.
- **Chapter 12 (Salvage) on the Account.** Kade's family legend says Fen took "something sealed" that "would have destroyed the Account", which tells us Hollis will get his hands on such a thing.
- **Chapter 18 (Salvage) on the Account.** This is the most premature case. Yara finds:
  - the vault opened;
  - the purser's privy box open and empty;
  - the Account torn from its cradle;
  - a white-haired body in a Chief Assessor's chain tied to the pedestal.

  That shows Marsh dying in the vault after opening the box, two chapters before Chapter 20 shows it. It is disguised (Yara assumes the body is Fen), but an attentive reader can identify Marsh from his hair and his chain.

### 4. Coherence and continuity: **8/10**
The plot's logic is very tight. Every mechanism of the finale is planted by Benn's recitation in Chapter 1:
- Article 1: key plus Book makes the purser.
- Article 9: the drop into the ballast.
- Article 11: an entry read aloud before witnesses stands as the Book.
- Article 14: the Master is whoever the Book records.
- Article 20: a debt discharged under seal is discharged forever.

The recovery port, the brandy in the privy box, the lead plate "ART. 9 — A.V.", and the voice that "shakes" on one word all pay off. The dates are exact: 929 − 611 = 318 Burnings, and 1317 − 611 = 706 years.

Flaws I noticed:
- **Ottilie's age doesn't add up.** Chapter 2 says she was 54 when twelve-year-old Hollis arrived 29 years earlier, which would make her about 83. Chapter 5 says she was 63.
- **Hollis's narration is a stretch.** His retrospective voice ("I've had a long time since to think") is revealed in Chapter 20 to be told within an hour at the top of the well. That is a bold device, and it strains belief across ten chapters of reflective prose.
- **One loose end.** Who later tore the Account out of the vault (the snapped bands in Chapter 18) is never explained.
- **Two convenient rulings.** Thorne's "lodging" rule and Rhee's acceptance of a radio-relayed seal under Article 11 both arrive when the plot needs them.
- **Thorne and Rhee are near-duplicates.** Both are "paper is paper" clerks. The echo is deliberate, but it is a little repetitive.

### 5. Craft: **9/10**
- **Prose.** It is exact, restrained and textured: "a voice shakes. That's why the Book keeps it"; the Index cards "drifting in a slow endless snowfall".
- **Character.** The book is morally serious without preaching:
  - Annick gives Marit Osei's name to save her sister, and the book never lets her off.
  - Tobiah is complicit in "somebody's got to pay for the air".
  - Marsh's line "every ledger is a forgery… the only question is who holds the pen" carries the theme.
- **Dialogue.** It is sharp and economical. Szell, Quarry, Calla and Mitya are each distinct.
- **Set pieces.** Marit at the lock, Mitya under the beam, and the knife fight in the spoke car are each superb.
- **Structure.** The three voices, the shared motifs (counting breaths, "four in, hold, four out", the level voice of the bill) and the planted Ordinances are expertly managed.
- **The ending.** Three sealed entries chain together, from "before witnesses. Who were asleep" to Yara's "Who were awake". The VOYAGE III plate in the Ash Church and "Don't sell it" close the book exactly.
- **Minor faults.** Some descriptions repeat (the Ash Case, the vault, the clerk-figures).

### 6. Overall: **9/10**
A complete, disciplined and moving novel that fully answers the brief. It leans on the future-myth device in a way that leaks the outline of earlier endings. Its prose and structure are first-rate.

## The second book (S)

### 1. Interlacing, three distinct eras and casts: **10/10**
- **Strict rotation.** Concession / Severance / Ebb from Chapter 1 to Chapter 21.
- **Fully separate casts:**
  - Teo, Nico, Ambrose, Corrigan, Vane and Salome in the Concession.
  - Juno, Pell, Adaeze, Mor, Rhee and Efua in the Severance.
  - Isra, Hanne, Pike, Lucan, the Prior and the Lamplighter in the Ebb.
- **Strong anchors:**
  - Ambrose's fork, "A. L. — 440 — FOR CLARE", becomes the Founder's Fork, "FOR CL".
  - The Tally.
  - The harp and the Lectern.
  - The buoy becomes "the Bell".
  - The beads that lean toward the mouth.
  - The Small Mercy and its delta crack.
- **Each era has its own social texture:** a company town, a theocratic wartime republic, and a post-collapse cult.
- **One weakness:** all three eras use the same close-third, past-tense register, so they are told apart by setting rather than by voice.

### 2. Synchronized pacing: **9/10**
- **Each era counts down to its own Narrowing** and the trough at its end: about 57 days to 1 May, 58 days to the Thirtieth Keeping, and 60 days to the Sixtieth.
- **The discoveries are mirrored in the same rotation:**
  - Chapters 7–9: the swell in the old traces, the Blank Year and the mismatch between the Fork and the harp, and the choke at the waist.
  - Chapters 13–15: the proof, the true pitch, and the strata.
- **The finale is in perfect order:** Chapter 19 (the Keeping and Vane's Henceforth order), Chapter 20 (the Thirtieth Keeping and the name read aloud), Chapter 21 (the throat clears and Teo's recording is played).
- **The build is even.** If anything, the three eras keep step more mechanically than the first book's.

### 3. No spoilers: **6/10**
The real secrets are held well:
- The First Warden's name is withheld until the Concession dramatizes it (Chapter 19) and then spoken in the Severance (Chapter 20).
- Teo's refusal to release her glass, and her fate, are held until Chapter 19 and heard in Chapter 21.

But the future's myths are transparent rather than misleading, and several later chapters give away an earlier era's answers early:

- **Chapter 2 (Severance) on the Concession.** The relic "Founder's Fork… FOR CL" comes one chapter after Ambrose's fork is shown engraved "FOR CLARE". So from Chapter 2 we know three things:
  - his fork ends up as the founder's relic, and he never brings it home to Clare;
  - a Keeping is instituted;
  - the Needle reopens ("twenty-nine times it has opened again").

  The Concession's central question ("can it close?") is answered here. And the Severance's climactic revelation in Chapter 20, "Not *for clarity*. *For Clare*", is something the reader has known for eighteen chapters.
- **Chapter 3 (Ebb) on the Concession and the Severance.** Hanne's history tells us the first Keeping "worked". It also tells us that the Severance ends in "the Quiet, and everything broke" and that the Keepings went on afterwards. The Ebb's whole premise, a Sixtieth Narrowing, tells us before the Severance begins its arc that the revelation in Chapter 20 does not end the Keeping for good.
- **Chapter 8 (Severance) on the Concession.** The Concession's harp records were "transferred to the keeping of the First Warden. Not returned", and the Fork and the harp's note disagree. This shows that Ambrose's evidence will be seized and the harp retuned, eleven chapters before Chapter 19 dramatizes it.
- **Chapter 9 (Ebb) on the Concession and the Severance.** The mass of Keeping glass choking the waist is revealed here. That comes before the Concession's "something stayed" (Chapter 10) and Ambrose's "sixty Keepings" sum (Chapter 16), and before Adaeze's "knot" (Chapter 14). The future reaches the past's key discovery first.
- **Chapter 14 (Severance) on the Concession.** The daybook line "Henceforth the harp is to be tuned each morning to the H.T. … By order of the F.W.", dated 2 May 2291, reproduces word for word the scene that closes Chapter 19.
- **Chapter 17 (Severance) on the Concession.** The Inheritance sets out the First Warden's plan in full: records destroyed, Keepers flown at the turn, a "sleeve name". It comes after Ambrose's death in Chapter 16 but before Chapter 19, so the Concession's last chapter mostly confirms what we already know.

### 4. Coherence and continuity: **8/10**
The internal arithmetic is careful:
- 2608 − 2291 = 317 years, which gives the Thirtieth Keeping.
- 646 years gives the Sixtieth.
- Ambrose's "sixty Keepings / 650 years" matches Isra and Lucan's count of fifty-nine bands.

The set-ups pay off:
- The socket in the buoy, where Ambrose seats the fork "like a key" in Chapter 4, takes the Fork in Chapter 21.
- Fennick's burst hand foreshadows glass shattering at its own note.
- Tobin's "I don't want to" is echoed by Pell.

Flaws I noticed:
- **The climax mechanism is more improvised than planted:**
  - a cut driver coil hidden under the harp;
  - the buoy's power port wired up by guesswork;
  - a ringing that turns the throat "inside out" to release 641 ships;
  - the Lamplighter's skiff found empty.
- **Two gaps are left unexplained.** Why the tellers kept the "old note" in the cittern's low string is never accounted for. Rusk returning the Fork is also left unexplained.
- **Small numerical slips:**
  - Juno's age at her commissioning is given as nineteen (Chapter 5) but implies twenty elsewhere (Chapters 2 and 5).
  - Teo's debt figures drift: 41,223, then 40,680, then 40,712.
  - Lucan says the harp was retuned "when the order lost the records", which conflicts with 2291.
- **Copy errors** such as "in her lap,." and "at the pocket,." in Chapter 11.

### 5. Craft: **8/10**
- **Prose.** It is warm and assured.
- **Set pieces.** Several are outstanding:
  - the canteen in the dark breathing with the Needle;
  - Corrigan's trial run;
  - Hanne's death on the salt;
  - the Prior retuning the harp on his knees to a string plucked two million kilometres away;
  - Pell turning round.
- **Character.** Hesper Vane's "every civilization is a story about what will happen if you stop" is a formidable villain's creed. Mor, the Prior and the Lamplighter are complicated believers rather than cartoons.
- **The ending.** Teo's recording, "Let it breathe", is genuinely moving, as are the Tally lines "PILOT / HARP / PILOT".
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Tics recur ("the way you would…", "she did not hurry", "a fat, frightened, decent man called Pryce" repeated word for word).
  - The same discovery is made almost three times: striking the Fork against the harp and hearing the beat (Adaeze in Chapter 8, Lucan in Chapter 9, Adaeze again in Chapter 14, Lucan again in Chapter 15).
  - Two Severance deaths turn on the same beat, a frightened subordinate firing unbidden (Marsh in Chapter 17, Adaeze in Chapter 20).
  - The three eras share one narrative voice.

### 6. Overall: **8/10**
A beautiful, humane and complete novel with excellent synchronization and an affecting ending. Its future eras give away the mechanics of the past too openly, and its climax relies more on improvised machinery than on payoffs planned from the start.

---

## Verdict

**The first book (T) is better. Strength of preference: clear.**

The three most important reasons:

1. **T keeps its secrets more cleverly.** Both books let later eras carry legends about earlier ones, but T's legends mislead:
   - Annick "the Purser who would have sold us";
   - "Fen the Turncoat";
   - "they burned the Book";
   - the white-haired body Yara takes for Fen.

   The finale overturns every one, so the reader is genuinely surprised. S's legends are transparent: "FOR CL" gives away Ambrose's fork in Chapter 2. And S's future eras rediscover and describe the First Warden's cover-up (Chapters 8, 14 and 17) and the choking glass (Chapter 9) before the Concession reaches them. The Severance's climactic reveal therefore lands as confirmation, not discovery.
2. **T's climax is earned from the first chapter.** Benn's recitation of the Ordinances in Chapter 1 (Articles 1, 9, 11, 14 and 20) is the exact machinery of the ending in all three eras. Key 0447, the privy box, VOYAGE III and the voice that shakes on one word all pay off. S's resolution depends on a hidden coil, guesswork wiring and a fantastical "ringing" that clears six centuries of glass. It is moving, but it is less inevitable.
3. **T's craft is sharper and more varied.** T gives each era its own voice: past-tense third person, first-person confession and present tense. Its moral centre (Annick's betrayal of Marit, Tobiah's complicity, "who holds the pen") is harder and less sentimental. S tells all three eras in one register, repeats its central discovery several times and reuses a death-by-panicked-gunman beat.

Both are strong, complete novels that meet the brief's structure. S matches T on interlacing and synchronization. T wins on spoiler-handling, plotting and prose.
