# Judgement: the first book (P) vs the second book (R)

Both books were read in full, every chapter. They are almost exactly the same length (about 97,500 and 98,000 words), so length plays no part in the comparison.

- **The first book (P)** follows the comet called the Lamp across three eras: the bonded crew of the skimmer *Patience* in 2391, a siege of the city of Patience in 2654, and a salvage run in 2917. All three are connected by one object, a Combine plague-weapon ("Asset Seven", the egg). Each era hides it on the Lamp, finds it there or destroys it there.
- **The second book (R)** follows the colony of Vigil across three eras: the strike and the Throat's closing in 2431, the debt-religion of the Count in 2794, and the Door reopening in 3190. All three are connected by the account HV-VIGIL-0001, the Gallows station, the halves of a broken surety tag and the motto "Count what's owed and not a mark more".

---

## The first book (P)

### 1. Interlacing, three distinct eras and casts: **10**
The chapters rotate strictly (Bonded / Siege / Last Return, Ch 1–21). The three casts never overlap: Maren, Abel, Ekene, Hale and Quist; Isaura, Malachy, Mattias, Corin and Dasha; Til, Juno, Oz, Hollis and Marchetti. Each era also has its own narrative mode: past-tense third person (2391), present-tense first person (2654) and present-tense third person (2917). You can tell which era you are in from the first sentence. Objects tie the eras together without crowding them: the drum names and the "seed with a tail" drawing (Ch 1 → Ch 2), the Share Plate, the Gull, the coat stencilled TSOLO, the line knife, and the saying "the Lamp takes the dead out and brings them back", which passes from Teska to the Lampers to the Roundsmen.

### 2. Synchronized pacing: **10**
The Lamp's closest pass is a real deadline in all three eras, so the arcs share a single clock. Each era's knowledge grows in step:
- Each era learns what the weapon is in three consecutive chapters: the egg in the cold hold (Ch 7), Mattias's "Asset Seven" card (Ch 11) and Hollis's loss register (Ch 12).
- The plan to put it on the Lamp is made (Ch 10, 13) at the same point that the Plate's orbit is decoded (Ch 11) and Til's "falling mountain" idea arrives (Ch 12).

The climaxes fall together: the throw (Ch 16), the landing under the black ridge (Ch 17) and the Roche break-up landing (Ch 18). The resolutions follow in order in Ch 19, 20 and 21. Ch 18 stops at the figure in the coat and holds back the cryostat and the Plate until era 2 has finished in Ch 20, which is very good control of what the reader knows.

### 3. No spoilers: **7**
Most of the shared material is handled as dramatic irony that pays off only after the earlier era reaches it. Era 2 hears "seized by mutineers" (Ch 11) and era 3 reads "17.xi.2391" (Ch 12) only after the seizure in Ch 10. Era 3 finds Abel and the Gull (Ch 18) only after era 2 has (Ch 17). Cases found:
- **Ch 2 (2654) spoils era 1's broad outcome (the significant one).** The free city of Patience exists, was "established by lawful purchase in 2391" by the Three Hundred, and the old skimmer was sunk into Kettle as the Spine. Before era 1's danger is even stated ("supply to cease", Ch 4), the reader knows the bonded keep the ship and the Camp survives to become a city of 180,000. The book recovers by making the "Purchase" a myth that it later overturns, but the survival question is gone.
- **Ch 2 and Ch 5 (2654), Blank Share: mild, deliberate.** The scraped first line at the top of the Plate is called "the Company's share". Once Ch 13 puts MAREN TSOLO first, an attentive reader can work out, six chapters early, that her name will be struck off (Ch 19).
- **Ch 3, 6 and 9 (2917): mild.** Oz's people left Corva "before the lane went dark", and nobody has come through in 200 years. The radar ping (Ch 6) and the light on Kettle (Ch 9) then hint that someone is still alive on Kettle. Together they suggest that the 2654 siege ends with the lane closed and the city not wiped out. The hints are kept ambiguous.
- **Ch 14 (2654), the Tether-Woman legend: not a spoiler.** The story that the tether-chief "went out with the Lamp" is a deliberate misdirection that sets up the switch to Abel in Ch 16.

### 4. Coherence and continuity: **9**
The plot is tightly locked together, and the later eras keep track of what the earlier ones left behind:
- Dasha's second anchor is still beside the shaft in 2917 (Ch 18).
- The knife is taken in Ch 17 and returned in Ch 20. Isaura's recorder is left next to Abel's, so Til finds two (Ch 21).
- The cryostat carries the scar from Corin's shot (Ch 17 → Ch 21).
- The anchor station that Grieve destroyed in Ch 20 is the debris field Til finds at the Throat in Ch 6.
- The dates hold: 263-year periods; the throw on 30 November and the register's 17.xi; "five hundred and twenty-six years".

Small slips:
- Ch 1 says Maren was "on the line for twenty-six [years] before" her nine as chief, which does not fit her age of 41. Elsewhere it is "twenty-six years on the line" in total.
- Hale's deal with Lachance (Ch 19) is convenient, though the book prepares for it.

### 5. Craft: **9**
The prose is precise and physical, from the tether's E-flat in the first line to the frozen accounts on Platform Four. The dialogue is spare and characterful: Teska, Szabo and Quist's "gardening". The moral turns are earned: Abel's drugged brandy and the throw ("Throw me straight"), Isaura cutting Corin's lanyard, and Til's "first time anybody said it right". The ending is outstanding. Til inherits the Plate, the knife and both recordings. The Air-Warden Ama Ocampo calls up with "There's air", and the lamps coming on across Kettle repeat Isaura turning every valve to the stop ("Breathe", Ch 20). Weaknesses:
- Repeated verbal tics: "like a dropped tray", "Teska says a lot of things", the "weather voice".
- Some passages are repeated almost word for word. The egg descriptions in Ch 7, 17 and 21 match, and so do the shaft descents in Ch 17 and 18.
- Til's account of the Harrow is told twice in full (Ch 15 to Oz, Ch 21 to Juno), which is close to padding.

### 6. Overall: **9**

## The second book (R)

### 1. Interlacing, three distinct eras and casts: **9**
The chapters rotate strictly (Bond / Ledger / Door, Ch 1–21). The three casts are distinct: Maren Tull, Pell, Dov, Hatch, Lucan Vey and Castellane; Ysolde, Anselm, Quist, Strand, Corin Asch and Piet; Rook, Wren, Haddie and Kask. The anchors are rich and well chosen: the motto, the tag halves, Hesper High becoming the Nave and then the Hulk, Gallows and the Hanged Men, the breathers' hymn with its wrong third line, the three knocks, and Teo Brand / Teodor Brand. The 2431 and 2794 strands share a narrative mode (past-tense third person), so only Rook's first-person 3190 strand has a voice of its own.

### 2. Synchronized pacing: **9**
The arcs build in parallel well:
- Each era meets the idea of Gallows within three chapters: the Gallows key and the inversion (Ch 10), the Compact's "Gallows" (Ch 11) and Haddie's "pins" (Ch 12).
- Three betrayals fall together: the failed farewell (Ch 13), the audit of Piet (Ch 14) and Kask's revelation about Jory (Ch 15).
- All three converge on the same round room at Gallows in Ch 19, 20 and 21, and the climaxes land one after another.

There is no shared clock like the Lamp. The deadlines are the convoy (era 1), the audit and the pursuit (era 2) and the Door opening (era 3), so the simultaneity is designed rather than felt. Era 3's middle (Ch 6–12) relies on chases and escapes more than the other two strands.

### 3. No spoilers: **6**
Several later-era chapters give away what era 1, and to a lesser degree era 2, has not yet reached:
- **Ch 2 (2794).** The Door is shut, there was a "Starving", and a debt-faith has kept the Count since the "Founding". The reader learns that the colony is cut off and left behind before era 1 even learns of the Exodus Clause (Ch 4).
- **Ch 2 and Ch 8 (2794), the First Auditor and the founding myth: the strongest case.** Quist's founding story (Ch 8) tells of twelve Stewards left in the Nave with thirty thousand people in the ice, and a quick child "in the Stewards' care" who wrote the first Count. Ch 4 has already said twelve officers stay as caretakers. Ch 1 has Pell's "numeric A+" and the shared motto, and the inscription by the First Auditor appears in Ch 2. Together these let a reader infer, around Ch 8, that the children were never shipped and that Pell became the First Auditor. Era 1's central question (do the children leave on the *Grace*?) is answered eleven chapters early.
- **Ch 11 (2794), the Compact.** "The Door is closed, and shall be taught as closed against them… No soul shall go to Gallows. What was done at Gallows is accounted and closed." It comes straight after Maren conceives of shutting the Throat "in front of them" (Ch 10). It signals that she will succeed at Gallows eight chapters before she does (Ch 19). The First Auditor's half-tag on the chair in the same chapter adds to it.
- **Ch 3 (3190).** The Door has stayed shut for the whole of history and only began to open 31 years ago; the Count-books are salvage and its faith survives only as an "old believers'" prayer. This hints, before era 2 begins in earnest, that Ysolde's era does not open the Door and that the Count eventually fades.
- **Ch 9 (3190), moderate.** The Hulk is the Nave, "dead for two hundred years", with the Count Board's plates fallen in a heap under the Ledger Hall's inscription. Ysolde's institution is shown collapsed while her arc is only at Ch 8.

Much of this is meant as dramatic irony, and Ch 20 still has real force. But more of the earlier eras' answers leak forward, and earlier, than in the first book.

### 4. Coherence and continuity: **8**
The causal chain is clever and mostly consistent:
- Collapse in 2431; restoration started in 2794 at "380–420 years"; the Door open by 3190.
- The Count beginning around 2453 fits Pell writing it as an adult.
- The ring reads "FACTOR SEAL ACCEPTED: CASTELLANE" (Ch 20), paying off the Factor's ring and "He kept the account".
- The *Mercy*'s pup is still docked in 3190 (Ch 21).

Problems:
- Ch 20 quotes the recording as saying "I hope it's you, Pell" near the end. In Ch 19 that line is not where Ch 20 places it.
- The Nave's death around 2990 is never accounted for.
- Rook's lottery roll also holds "four hundred and twelve" children (Ch 18). The echo strains belief.
- The receiver accepts a sealed reconciliation and hands itself over "in settlement of balance" (Ch 21). The book sets this up, but it reads as a bureaucratic deus ex machina.
- Wren's killing of Kask and Haddie's death are both deliberately kept off the page. That works once, but Rook's "I'll not tell you" becomes a habit.

### 5. Craft: **8**
The prose is strong and often lyrical. Highlights:
- Era 1: the dump-lever downdraft (Ch 1), the farewell "without the glass" and the hand sliding out "like a rope going down into the red" (Ch 13), Dov's confession and the chit dropped out of the vent (Ch 16), and Maren's recording to Pell with the three knocks (Ch 19).
- Era 2: the Count Board Turning and the Reliquary are memorable set pieces.
- The two tag halves fitted together over a faint "0212" (Ch 20) is the single best image in either book.

Weaknesses:
- Era 3 is weaker. Kask is a flat villain; the drones, pods and tenders are generic SF; and Rook's refrains ("I'll not tell you", "I'll be honest") are overused.
- The Gallows recording is presented three times (Ch 19, 20, 21), which is close to repetition.
- The pattern of the protector's sacrifice (Lucan, Corin, Haddie, Dov) recurs with little variation.

### 6. Overall: **8**

## Verdict

**The first book (P) is better. Strength of preference: clear.**

Three most important reasons:

1. **Pacing is synchronized through one shared object.** In P the Lamp is literally the same body coming back. Each era's choice puts the egg in the next era's path, and all three arcs race the same perihelion, so the reveals (Ch 10–12) and climaxes (Ch 16–18) happen together by construction. The order of reveals is handled with great care: the cryostat and the Plate are held back in Ch 18 until era 2 resolves. R's eras are linked causally just as well, but they run on separate deadlines.
2. **Fewer and later spoilers.** P gives away one big thing early: the city exists (Ch 2). R lets the shut Door (Ch 2), the twelve Stewards and the child who wrote the Count (Ch 8), and the Compact's "what was done at Gallows" (Ch 11) answer era 1's central questions well before Ch 19. R's 3190 strand also shows the Nave dead (Ch 9) while era 2 is still mid-arc.
3. **More even craft across all three strands, with an ending that brings them together.** P gives each era its own narrative mode, and its third era (Til, Juno, Oz, Marchetti) is as strong as the other two. Its finale passes the Plate, the knife and the recordings forward and closes on Kettle's lights repeating Isaura's valves. R has the single finest moment, the fused tag, but its third era leans on generic machinery, a flat villain and an overused narrative tic, and its resolution runs on a machine accepting the receipt.
