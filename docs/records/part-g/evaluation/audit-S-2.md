# Continuity audit: book S

1. **The long tide's turn moves from late April to 11 April**
   - Chapters: 7, 13
   - Ch 7: "the long tide would turn somewhere in the last days of April." Also: "watch the swell's period, every night, for the next five weeks", and "it would take five weeks to know".
   - Ch 13: "The swell slowed on the eleventh of April at nine minutes past three in the morning, exactly where the sum on the back of the envelope had said it would." Later in the same chapter: "the long tide underneath it had turned."
   - Why both can't be true: The sum Ambrose did in Ch 7 put the turn at the end of April, and he expected it to take five weeks to confirm. Ch 13 says the turn came almost three weeks earlier, "exactly" where that same sum predicted. The plot needs the early date (the proof has to exist before he sails on 28 April), but it contradicts the prediction it claims to fulfil.
   - Severity: **serious**

2. **"Nobody alive had seen" a Keeper's skiff flown**
   - Chapters: 3, 6, 15, 18
   - Ch 3: Isra's Return is "A Keeper's skiff, the narrow kind … the kind they had flown in the Keepings before the Quiet, Hanne said, though nobody alive had seen one flown." It is "Twelve metres nose to tail."
   - Ch 6: Pike flew escort at the Fifty-Ninth, eleven years earlier: "We took the Keepers out to the mouth. Seven skiffs." Mercy says: "Nine skiffs at the Fifty-Eighth. Seven at the Fifty-Ninth."
   - Ch 15, Book of Keepings: "*Fifty-Eighth: nine.*"
   - Ch 18, Tobin's Fifty-Ninth Return: "A skiff. Small. Twelve metres. A Keeper's skiff."
   - Why both can't be true: Skiffs of the same twelve-metre design were flown 11 and 22 years earlier, in front of escorts, Pike and the whole Lantern. Hanne (73) was alive for both Keepings.
   - Severity: **minor**

3. **How long Cassius Mor has been High Warden: 19 years or 25**
   - Chapters: 2, 5, 17 (also 20)
   - Ch 5: "He had declined every one for nineteen years." Adaeze adds: "He has declined every petition for substitution since he was invested."
   - Ch 2: "Cassius Mor was sixty-six".
   - Ch 17: he read the Inheritance "at forty-one … And then he was High Warden for twenty-five years." In Ch 20 he says: "I've believed that for twenty-five years."
   - Why both can't be true: 66 − 41 = 25, and "the last three Draws" (Ch 5) also needs about 25 years in office. Nineteen years would cover only two Draws.
   - Severity: **minor**

4. **Adaeze could not have written the petitions at three Draws**
   - Chapters: 5, 8
   - Ch 5: "Two hundred and six of them, at the last three Draws. I've written every one." The petitions came to her "because she was the archivist."
   - Ch 8: "Adaeze had read them at the Twenty-Ninth, eleven years ago, her first Keeping as archivist". Also: "Adaeze had laughed at the joke, at twenty-four" (as a novice). In Ch 5 she is 44 and has "served here for twenty years".
   - Why both can't be true: The Draw before the Twenty-Ninth (about 2586) came before she joined the order (at 24, in about 2588), let alone before she became archivist. She could have written petitions at two Draws at most.
   - Severity: **trivial**

5. **Juno's age at her commissioning**
   - Chapters: 2, 5
   - Ch 5, Adaeze: "I saw you, once, at your commissioning. You were nineteen."
   - Ch 2: Pell was "six and Juno was eighteen and leaving for the Academy". Pell: "we went up to the Anchorhold for your commissioning … I was eight."
   - Ch 5, the Rhee backstory: "At the Twenty-Ninth Draw, eleven years ago, when Juno was twenty and Rhee was twenty …" Rhee then "stayed quiet through their last year, and two days after commissioning" deserted.
   - Why both can't be true: Pell being eight makes Juno 20 at the commissioning, and the Rhee timeline puts it after she turned 20. Nineteen fits neither.
   - Severity: **trivial**

6. **"Six weeks parked at the Anchorhold"**
   - Chapters: 5, 8, 11, 14
   - Ch 5: the *Vigil* is posted "with station at the Anchorhold from the twentieth of November until the Narrowing." Marsh reads this as "Six weeks parked at the Anchorhold guarding the glass stores and then we fly the Keepers".
   - The Keeping is on 5 December. Ch 8: "the twenty-eighth of October, with thirty-eight days to go". Ch 11: "the fifth of December". Ch 14: "the seventeenth of November, eighteen days before the Thirtieth Keeping".
   - Why both can't be true: 20 November to 5 December is about two weeks, not six.
   - Severity: **minor**

7. **Two sleeve-name reasons are dated after the Draws they explain**
   - Chapters: 5, 8
   - Ch 8, Teague's list: "*Twenty-Sixth: Orla Venning … voted against the Wardens' appropriation, 2573*" and "*Twenty-Seventh: Hal Brisk … moved the motion for Shoals quota relief, 2584*".
   - Ch 5: "the Twenty-Ninth Draw, eleven years ago", which is 2597. Draws are "every four thousand days" (about 11 years).
   - Why both can't be true: Counting back, the Twenty-Sixth Draw was about 2564 and the Twenty-Seventh about 2575. Each "reason" comes after the Draw it supposedly caused (9 years after for Venning, 9 for Brisk), so these are punishments handed out before the offence. The Twenty-Eighth and Twenty-Ninth entries line up correctly.
   - Severity: **minor**

8. **The date Ambrose first heard the swell**
   - Chapters: 1, 4, 13
   - Ch 1: The Lantern broadcast is "on the night of the second of March". At two in the morning that night Ambrose writes "*probably instrumental*". In Ch 4 Vane says: "I told you so on the second."
   - Ch 13: "the little column in his notebook that had begun on the night of the first of March with the word *probably*."
   - Why both can't be true: The Narrowing was first seen on 2 March, so the note was written that night, not on the night of the 1st.
   - Severity: **trivial**

9. **"Another eighteen days" counted from 11 April**
   - Chapter: 13
   - "Not the waist. The waist would go on narrowing for another eighteen days". This is on the night of the 11th, and the trough is set for "the first of May".
   - Two days later in the canteen (the 13th), Ambrose again says "eighteen days".
   - Why both can't be true: From 11 April to 1 May is 20 days. Eighteen is only right from the 13th.
   - Severity: **trivial**

10. **How fast the sound recording is sped up**
    - Chapters: 13, 21
    - Ch 13: "Then he sped it up. A thousand times. So that a month of the long tide went by in forty minutes, and three hours in eleven seconds."
    - In the canteen (Ch 13), and on the recording (Ch 21): "Forty days of it, in forty minutes."
    - Why both can't be true: At 1,000×, forty minutes is about 28 days (the "month" is right). Forty days in forty minutes would need about 1,440×.
    - Severity: **trivial**

11. **The order of the bands in the mass**
    - Chapter: 15
    - First description: two thick bands on top, then "in the middle, a long run of medium bands … thirty or so … and then, below those, nearer the bottom, a stretch where the bands thinned out again and became irregular and faint".
    - When Lucan checks against the Book: under the two thick bands come the Fifty-Seventh to Fifty-Fifth, "Three bands — and the third … so faint". Then: "Below the Forty-Second … the long run of medium bands went on down, steady, thirty-odd of them". Then the single thin first band at the very bottom.
    - Why both can't be true: By the Book, the faint, irregular Quiet-era bands sit between the thick top and the medium run. The first description puts them below the medium run, where no Keepings could lie.
    - Severity: **trivial**

12. **Band thickness doesn't match the skiff counts**
    - Chapters: 2, 3, 11, 15, 19
    - Ch 15: the Fifty-Eighth ("*nine*") is one of two bands "very thick, laid down on everything else like two coats of paint". The Compact-era Keepings, also nine skiffs ("Nine are asked", Ch 2), give a run of "medium bands". The bottom band, read as the First, is "Very thin. Almost nothing."
    - Ch 19: the First Keeping had eight tugs, "each cradle loaded with two tonnes", released at the waist, on top of Corrigan's two tonnes from the trial.
    - Ch 11: a Compact Keeping uses "eighteen tonnes" for nine.
    - Ch 3: the Lantern only went beyond "a cradle each" at the Sixtieth.
    - Why both can't be true: The text says band thickness tracks how many skiffs flew, yet nine cradles appear as both "very thick" and "medium". The First's 18 tonnes, the same as a Compact Keeping, appears as "almost nothing".
    - Severity: **minor**

13. **Where the penitents' cells are**
    - Chapters: 4, 5, 17
    - Ch 17, Hollis: "The penitents' cells. The old Concession lock-up. Bottom of the spindle, Needle-ward end, under the docks." Later: "The High Warden's gallery was at the top of the spindle, the far end of the station."
    - Ch 4: "The Director's Gallery was at the top of the station's spindle, the Needle-ward end". Ch 5: "at the very top of the Anchorhold's spindle, at the Needle-ward end".
    - Why both can't be true: The Needle-ward end is the top, where the gallery is. The cells can't be at the bottom and at the Needle-ward end, and also at the far end from the gallery.
    - Severity: **trivial**

14. **Nine pilots, but only eight named**
    - Chapters: 16, 19
    - Ch 19: "There were nine tugs on Dock Four … and there were nine pilots standing in front of them in a line". The list is Haldane, the two Moss brothers, Ida Okonjo, Jory Ferris, Big Sven and Marguerite, "And Corrigan's empty place, which nobody stood in, and then Teo. Nine. Eight debts, and Nico." Salome blesses seven by name, then Teo. Bettany later calls "nine tugs into the throat and eight out of it".
    - Ch 16, Vane: "I have eight pilots. I need nine."
    - Why both can't be true: Seven named pilots plus Teo is eight. "Eight debts, and Nico" needs eight other pilots, and Corrigan's empty place isn't a pilot or a tug.
    - Severity: **minor**

15. **The fork is struck at 4 a.m., while the Keepers are still on the dock**
    - Chapter: 19
    - "There were nine tugs on Dock Four at four in the morning on the first of May … nine pilots standing in front of them". The blessing, the Director's speech and the flight out through the shear all follow.
    - Later: the fork "had been struck at four in the morning on the first of May, from the gallery above the dome, by the Director's own hand, at the moment the Keepers went into the mouth".
    - Why both can't be true: At four the Keepers were standing on Dock Four. They reached the mouth well after that.
    - Severity: **minor**

16. **The *Ardour* is in two places**
    - Chapter: 20
    - "Home Squadron … coming up from Tallow. The *Constant*, the *Resolute*, the *Ardour*, six frigates." The League's nineteen ships then hold the line "between the Anchorhold and the Home Squadron".
    - Shortly after, at the Keeping barge by the mouth: "the *Ardour* sat beside them as their escort with her forward guns out."
    - Why both can't be true: The *Ardour* arrives with the squadron that the League is holding back on the far side of the station. Nothing explains how she is also at the barge.
    - Severity: **trivial**

17. **The skiff counts don't add up to nine**
    - Chapter: 20
    - Pell's is "the third light". Then "a fourth light turned. Abbe Sorrell. Then a fifth. Then a sixth. Three did not." The returning skiffs: "The sixth skiff came in alongside the *Vigil*, and then the fifth and the fourth, and last of all the third".
    - Why both can't be true: Four turn back (the third to sixth lights) and three go on, which is seven of the nine Keepers. Two are left out. Reading "a sixth" as six turned would add up, but it clashes with the four skiffs that come home.
    - Severity: **trivial**

18. **When the door opened, and when the last three went in**
    - Chapter: 20
    - At 3 a.m. on 5 December, Greaves: "The Keeping flies in four hours." The readers then call the Keepers in during the broadcast that morning ("Waist's turning … They're calling the Keepers in"), and three skiffs go on into the mouth.
    - Later: "The door opened at thirty-one minutes past seven on the morning of the sixth of December … two hours after the last three Keepers went in."
    - Why both can't be true: The last three went in on the morning of the 5th. Two hours after that is the 5th, not 7:31 on the 6th, about a day later.
    - Severity: **minor**

19. **The First Warden's book changes its name**
    - Chapters: 17, 20
    - Ch 17: "They call it the Inheritance". Ch 20 also has "had stolen the Inheritance".
    - Ch 20: "she had read every page of the Testament but the last".
    - Why both can't be true: The sealed book is called the Inheritance everywhere else. "Testament" appears only once, with no reason given.
    - Severity: **trivial**

Counts by severity: serious 1, minor 9, trivial 9 (19 in all).
