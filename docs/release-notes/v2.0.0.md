Sixty-one changes since v1.1.0, and a new name: PlotWeave is now **Kathala**.
Most of these changes came from novelists writing whole books in the app and
reporting each thing that got in their way.

**Why 2.0.** How a story's state is stored has changed underneath. A scene now
records only what it says, where a record used to hold a full copy of everyone's
state. Your worlds upgrade by themselves the first time 2.0 opens them, and
backups made by any earlier version still import. **A backup made by 2.0 will
not open in 1.x**, so update every copy of the app you use before trading files
between them.

## The scene header

A scene's header is now a block at the top of the Page, written in plain lines,
and Kathala keeps the records in step with it:

- **Place** and **Characters**, with `:dead` and `:alive` for a death or a
  return, and a new name or a reveal written on the Characters line.
- **Items**, with an item's condition. **Facts**, for what someone learns.
  **Relationships**, with `:ended`.
- **When:**, for the date a scene happens.
- **A cheat sheet** for the syntax sits beside the Page and the scene card.
- **Who is in a scene, suggested from who speaks in it.** A suggestion appears
  only where it held up when tested on the Library's books. Hand-offs and deaths
  are not suggested, because they did not hold up.
- **Pasted header blocks are headers from the start**, and stay headers after a
  reload. A place or a person named in the header can be made from it, with no
  map needed and their other names asked for.

## Time, dates and eras

- **A date for every scene**, given in dates rather than day counts. You choose,
  per book, whether a pinned date sets the clock.
- **Eras.** Years can count down to a turning point and then count up again, so a
  book can run from *19 BBY* down to *0 ABY*, and later eras count up again
  from a number you choose.
- **Leap years**, with your own rule for which years gain days and where.
  Weekdays are supported too.
- **A book braided from timelines** is numbered as one book. A chapter can move
  anywhere in it, timelines appear in the order you set, and the Page can show
  every timeline at once.
- What happens in one era never overwrites another.
- **A scene that opens later than its dates allow is flagged**: *"A fortnight
  later"*, when the dates say three days.

## Characters' names over the book

- **Names that change**, and other names a reader learns at a set scene.
- **Characters revealed to be the same person.** Strider can turn out to be
  Aragorn. Before the reveal a reader sees two people, and from the reveal on,
  one.

## Spoilers

- **Spoiler words.** A fact can list the words that would give it away. The
  checker warns when one appears before the scene where the reader is meant to
  learn it, and **the Page underlines it as you type**. In a braided book, the
  underline names the other timeline's chapter where the reveal happens.
- **What the reader knows, while you write**, scene by scene, across a braided
  book.
- The warnings now match spoiler words only, not a fact's tags. In one book,
  tags had caused three warnings in four.

## The AI audit

Some continuity errors can only be found by reading the prose.
**Audit with AI** hands the whole book to an assistant of your choice: the
prose, each chapter's era and dates, the fact schedule and birth dates.

- **Copy the prompt** into any assistant, or **run it here with your own key**:
  Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or DeepSeek. The key goes only to that provider. In the
  desktop app it can be stored, encrypted by your system's keychain.
- **Every quote is checked against the book.** A finding whose quotes are not in
  your text is dropped before you see it. A finding goes away once you rewrite
  the words it quotes.
- **A chapter at a time**, checked against the book so far.
- It finds contradictions in numbers and time, and **early reveals**, including
  those told through legend, rumour, a relic or a later era's history.

## Nothing lost

- **Closing the page loses nothing.** A test closes the page partway through
  each kind of save and checks what survives. Unsaved prose, a split or join
  being written, and headings or header lines typed but not yet applied all come
  back when you reopen the book.
- **A correction carries forward.** Who holds an item, and where a person is,
  are recorded once, at the scene where they change. Fixing an earlier scene
  updates everything after it, where older copies used to outvote it.
- **Faster on long books.** Screens no longer do work for panels nobody has
  opened.

## Smaller things

Find & replace keeps line breaks, and Replace all happens in one step. A world
backup downloads even when the app is run by an automated browser. Two places can
share a name. Indented verse keeps its line breaks. The Page stays in its own world, and the
chapter panel follows its chapter. The caret stays where it was when focus
arrives from elsewhere. And the Library's names were checked against the books
themselves.
