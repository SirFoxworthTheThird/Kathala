# Wuthering Heights manuscript plan

## Authoritative edition

- Emily Brontë, *Wuthering Heights*.
- Project Gutenberg eBook #768, plain-text edition, updated 6 May 2026.
- Source: <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/768>
- The edition contains 34 numbered, untitled chapters. Any descriptive chapter or scene labels in PlotWeave are editorial signposts and will be disclosed as such in Lore.

## Lossless scene policy

The complete public-domain narrative is divided into consecutive scene drafts. Scene boundaries may be inserted only where place, present cast, narrator level, knowledge, ownership, relationship state, or dramatic tension materially changes. The build must mechanically prove that concatenating every scene draft reproduces the normalized source narrative exactly, with no omission, duplication, or reordering.

## Chronology policy

Use one chronology. Lockwood's 1801–1802 present and Nelly Dean's reconstruction of 1771 onward belong to the same story chronology even though the telling is nested. Event dates represent when the narrated action occurs, not merely when Nelly recounts it. Approximate dates must be identified as editorial reconstruction in Lore.

## Spatial model

The minimum useful hierarchy is:

- Yorkshire moors root map
  - Wuthering Heights grounds and house
  - Thrushcross Grange grounds and house
  - Gimmerton and its churchyard

Every child layer requires one accurately placed parent gateway and book-specific locations. Deep nesting and playback transitions must be checked in PlotWeave after generation.
