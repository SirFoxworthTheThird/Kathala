# Journey to the West — remaining art

Consolidated after reading all 100 chapters. 50 images are already in
`public/library/journey-to-the-west/`. This is everything still missing,
ranked, plus four fixes to images already supplied.

House style for all of it: the coloured-ink painting style of the character
plates already supplied (ink linework, colour wash, plain or lightly textured
ground). Landscape 3:2 for maps and places, portrait 2:3 for figures, square
for items on a plain ground. Please avoid Roman lettering anywhere in frame.

---

## 0. Fixes to images already supplied

These four are usable but say the wrong thing. Replacing them is worth more
than any single new picture.

**0.1 `art/items/golden-fillet.jpg`** — the plate is labelled 金蓮冠, a lotus
crown. The book's object is a plain circlet: a thin gold band, no ornament, no
peaks, sized to a man's head, that tightens when a spell is recited. Draw the
band alone. If it carries any label it should be 金箍.

**0.2 `art/items/purple-gold-bells.jpg`** — the plate shows a single hung
temple bell. The book has **three** small hand-bells on a cord, each the size
of a teacup or a fist, wrapped in a leopard-skin cloth with cotton stuffed in
their mouths. Fire comes out of the first, smoke out of the second, sand out
of the third. Draw the three together, stoppered, on the unrolled cloth.

**0.3 `art/places/purple-cloud-kingdom.jpg`** — the banner in frame reads
紫云國. The kingdom is 朱紫國, Purple Cinnabar. Same scene, corrected
characters.

**0.4 `art/places/lotus-cave.jpg`** — reads as a quiet hillside shrine. It is
the lair of Gold Horn and Silver Horn: a cave mouth in Pingding Mountain with
smoke, weapon-racks, and demon-guards on the path. Same composition is fine,
darker weather and armed sentries.

## Tier 1 — the things the book cannot be shown without

**1.1 The gold-banded cudgel (如意金箍棒), item.**
The single most recognisable object in the novel and we have no plate of it.
An iron staff with a gold band at each end, dark iron shaft, shown at its
resting length (about the height of a man). Originally the measuring rod that
set the depth of the seas; weighs 13,500 catties. Plain ground.

**1.2 The nine-toothed rake (九齒釘鈀), item.**
Zhu Bajie's. A heavy nine-toothed rake head on a long shaft, 5,048 catties,
made in Heaven of divine ice-iron with gold banding. Not a farm tool — the
teeth are drooping fangs, the shaft is bound.

**1.3 The demon-quelling staff (降妖寶杖), item.**
Sha Wujing's. A monk's fighting staff cut from a moon-laurel branch, banded
with gold at intervals along its length, 5,048 catties. Slimmer than the
cudgel, with a slight taper.

**1.4 The nine-ring pilgrim staff (九環錫杖), item.**
Tripitaka's, one of Guanyin's three gifts. A tall ringed staff, nine loose
metal rings hanging from the finial, no blade — it is not a weapon.

**1.5 The purple-gold alms bowl (紫金缽盂), item.**
Put into his hands by Emperor Taizong at the gate of Chang'an, carried the
whole road, and given up at Vulture Peak as the "gift" that buys the
scriptures with words in them. A shallow gold-purple begging bowl.

**1.6 The White Bone Demon (白骨精), character.**
The most famous episode in the book and we have nothing for it. She takes
three shapes in one chapter — a girl carrying a rice pot, an old woman, an old
man — and what is left after the third blow is a heap of bones lettered with
her name. Best treatment: one plate showing the girl with the rice pot, with
her true skeleton faintly behind or beneath her. White Tiger Ridge, bare rock.

**1.7 Subodhi the Patriarch (須菩提祖師), character.**
The teacher on the Mountain of Mind and Heart who gives Wukong his name, the
seventy-two transformations and the cloud-somersault, and then forbids him
ever to say who taught him. Everything the monkey can do begins here. An old
Daoist master on a high seat in a cave-hall, students seated below.

**1.8 Erlang Shen (二郎顯聖真君), character.**
The only one who ever beat Wukong in a fair contest, and the one who comes
back to help him at Green Wave Pool. A young warrior god in armour, three eyes
(the vertical eye in the forehead), three-pointed two-edged spear, with the
slender celestial hound at his heel and a hawk on the wrist.

**1.9 Nezha, Third Prince (哪吒三太子), character.**
Appears in more chapters than any other heavenly figure — the havoc in Heaven,
the Rhinoceros King, the Bull Demon King, the lawsuit. A boy in armour, six
arms in his combat form, with fire-wheels under his feet, the universe-ring,
the red silk sash, and the demon-slaying sabre.

**1.10 Li Jing, the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King (托塔李天王), character.**
Nezha's father, commander of the heavenly troops. Bearded general in armour,
the small many-storeyed gold pagoda balanced on his left palm — he carries it
because he is afraid of his own son.

**1.11 The three kings of Lion Camel Ridge, character (one plate, three figures).**
The most dangerous arc in the book, four chapters long. Left: a blue-haired
lion (Manjusri's mount) who swallows a hundred thousand troops in a mouthful.
Centre: a white elephant with long tusks (Samantabhadra's) whose trunk crushes
iron-backed men. Right, largest: the Great Roc of Ten Thousand Li, gold-winged,
the Buddha's own kin — with the yin-yang vase at his belt.

**~~1.12 The cover plate~~ — done, and not by you.** The catalogue card now
shows a leaf of the 1592 Shidetang first edition: Sun Wukong with the cudgel
outside the Plantain Cave. It is public domain and it is from the book itself,
which beats anything commissioned. If you would rather the card carried your
own house style — the four pilgrims and the horse on a mountain road,
portrait, croppable to 2:3 — say so and it can be swapped; otherwise this one
is closed.

## Tier 2 — arcs of three chapters or more with no picture at all

**2.1 The Yellow Robe Monster (黃袍怪), character.** Chapters 28–31. A demon
lord of Bowyue Cave; in truth Kui Wood Wolf, one of the twenty-eight lodges of
Heaven, absent four roll-calls. Show him in his handsome-lordly human form at
the court of the Precious Elephant Kingdom, with the wolf's shadow.

**2.2 Sai Tai Sui (賽太歲), character.** Chapters 68–71. Guanyin's golden-haired
*hou* — a lion-like beast with a mane of gold thread — that bit through its
chain and came down to take the Golden Queen. Show the beast, with the three
bells on a cord at its neck.

**2.3 The Yellow Brow Great King (黃眉大王), character.** Chapters 65–66. Maitreya's
cymbal-striking boy, who set up a false Little Thunderclap Monastery and sat in
the Buddha's seat. A round-faced, yellow-browed figure in a Buddha's robes on a
lotus dais, holding a short wolf-tooth club (really a bell-hammer), with an old
white cloth wallet at his hip.

**2.4 Lady Earth-Gusher (地湧夫人), character.** Chapters 80–83. The Gold-Nosed
White-Haired Mouse Spirit, who stole the Buddha's incense and candles three
hundred years ago and was spared. A beautiful woman in the Bottomless Cave, a
white mouse's nose and whiskers just visible; two swords; a plain altar behind
her holding two tablets.

**2.5 The three Daoist immortals of Cart-Slow (虎力、鹿力、羊力大仙), character
(one plate, three figures).** Chapters 44–46. Tiger Power, Deer Power and Goat
Power, court preceptors who enslaved five hundred monks. Three Daoists in
ceremonial robes on an altar platform; behind or beneath each, faintly, a
yellow tiger, a white antlered deer, a goat.

**2.6 The State Preceptor and the Beauty (國丈與美后), character.** Chapters
78–79. The Bhiksu Kingdom: an old Daoist with a dragon-headed staff and a
sixteen-year-old girl, who between them convinced a king he needed the hearts
of 1,111 boys. He is the Old Man of the South Pole's white deer; she is a
white-faced fox. Show the pair, with the animal natures faint behind them.

**2.7 The Queen of West Liang (西梁女王), character.** Chapter 54. The one
temptation the book takes entirely seriously and never mocks: a sovereign
offering a whole kingdom, in good faith, to a man who cannot take it. A queen
in full court dress on a phoenix carriage, her hand out.

**2.8 The Five Elements Mountain (五行山), place.** Where the monkey spends five
hundred years under the Buddha's seal, and where the journey actually begins. A
mountain with a stone box at its foot, a monkey's head and hands visible, the
gold seal-paper with six characters (唵嘛呢叭𡄰吽) pasted on the summit rock,
moss and pine growing over him.

**2.9 The Flaming Mountains (火焰山), place.** Chapters 59–61. Eight hundred li
of fire, no grass, red brick and red-tiled farms in the foreground, sixty li
short of the fire line. The fire came from bricks kicked out of Laozi's furnace.

**2.10 The Lion Camel City (獅駝城), place.** Chapters 74–77. A walled city whose
king and people were eaten five hundred years ago and replaced; black air over
the walls, demon soldiers on the ramparts in human armour. The horror is that it
looks like a normal capital until you are close.

**2.11 The Water Curtain Cave (水簾洞), place.** The origin place, and the only
home Wukong has. An iron-plate bridge under a waterfall, the falls hanging like
a white curtain over the cave mouth, stone furniture inside, the stone tablet
reading 花果山福地，水簾洞洞天.

**2.12 The Cloud-Reaching Ford and the bottomless boat (凌雲渡), place.** Chapter
98. The last crossing before Vulture Peak: eight or nine li of fast water, a
single round log for a bridge, and a boat with no bottom poled by the Buddha Who
Leads Across. A corpse floats down the current past it. This is the climax of the
whole road and we have nothing for it.

## Tier 3 — worth having, in rough order

**3.1 A map of the four continents.** The book's cosmology, which the route map
cannot show: Jambudvipa (south, the Tang lands), Purvavideha (east, Flower-Fruit
Mountain), Aparagodaniya (west, the Buddha's land), Uttarakuru (north), around
Mount Sumeru, with the four seas and their dragon palaces. Painted as a
Ming-dynasty world diagram rather than a modern map.

**3.2 The Ten Kings of the Underworld (十殿閻君), character.** Chapters 3, 11,
58, 97 — the book returns to the underworld four times. Ten crowned judges in
court robes at a long bench, the Register of Life and Death open, the Wheel of
Rebirth behind.

**3.3 Ao Guang, Dragon King of the Eastern Sea (東海龍王敖廣), character.**
Chapters 3, 43, 45, 69. A crowned dragon-king in court dress with dragon
features, in the crystal palace.

**3.4 Manjusri and Samantabhadra (文殊、普賢), character (one plate).** The two
bodhisattvas whose mounts cause two separate disasters, and who come to collect
them. One on a blue lion, one on a white elephant.

**3.5 The Fish-Basket Guanyin (魚籃觀音), character.** Chapter 49 gives the
origin of this famous image: she comes out of the bamboo grove unpainted, hair
undressed, carrying a basket she has just split and woven, and a village
painter takes her likeness. Bamboo basket, one goldfish in it.

**3.6 The Bottomless Cave (無底洞), place.** Chapters 80–83. A boulder on
Empty-Trap Mountain with a hole in it the width of a jar's mouth, worn smooth;
three hundred li straight down; and inside, its own sky, wind and flowering
trees.

**3.7 The Golden Lamp Bridge (金燈橋), place.** Chapter 91. Lantern Festival
night at Jinping: three lamp-vats the size of water butts on a bridge, filigree
gold two-storey lantern-houses over them, crowds, and three false Buddhas
descending on the wind.

**3.8 The Wooden Immortal Hermitage (木仙庵), place.** Chapter 64. Moonlight on
Thorn Ridge, a stone hut, four old gentlemen and an apricot fairy at a table
composing linked verse — pine, cypress, juniper and bamboo faintly behind them.
The one night of the journey Tripitaka is treated as a poet.

**3.9 The Diamond Jade Bracelet (金剛琢), item.** Chapters 50–52. A plain bright
white-gold ring that swallows any weapon thrown at it — made at the Han Pass,
and later run through an ox's nose.

**3.10 The Human-Seed Bag and the golden cymbals (人種袋、金鐃), item (one
plate).** Chapters 65–66. An old white cloth wallet, and a pair of gold
cymbals two feet across that seal shut over a man with no seam.

**3.11 The peaches of immortality (蟠桃), item.** The Queen Mother's garden:
three thousand six hundred trees, ripening in three ranks at three thousand,
six thousand and nine thousand years. A branch with fruit, on a plain ground.

**3.12 The Great King of Miraculous Response (靈感大王), character.** Chapters
47–49. Guanyin's goldfish from the lotus pond, grown huge on sermons, with a
nine-petalled copper mace that is really an unopened lotus bud. Took a boy and a
girl from Chen Village every year for the rain.

## Tier 4 — only if you are enjoying it

- The Scorpion Demon of Pipa Cave (ch 55) — she stung the Buddha's thumb.
- The three rhinoceroses of Green Dragon Mountain (ch 91–92) — Cold-Avoiding,
  Heat-Avoiding, Dust-Avoiding, whose horns part water.
- Vairambha of the Thousand Flower Cave (ch 73) — an old woman whose only
  weapon is an embroidery needle forged in her son's eye.
- Zhenwu of Wudang with the turtle and snake (ch 66).
- The Jetavana / Gold-Spread Monastery (ch 93) — the ground bought by covering
  it in gold bricks, which still washes up gold after rain.
- Gao Village (ch 18–19) — where Bajie was a son-in-law.
- The Yellow Wind Ridge cave (ch 20–21).
