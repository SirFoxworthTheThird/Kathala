91.2 three-buddhas-on-the-bridge | Three Buddhas Come Down to the Lamps | golden-lamp-bridge | tripitaka, wukong, three-rhinoceros-demons | The wind rises before the third watch and everyone runs, because the Buddhas are coming. The one man who runs towards them is the pilgrim, and he is up on the bridge bowing when the lights go dark.
91.3 three-goats | Three Rams for Three Yangs | green-dragon-mountain | wukong, four-duty-officers | Four men driving three sheep down the west slope shouting "Kai-tai" — the Year, Month, Day and Hour officers, arranging three yangs opening the tai hexagram to break his master's ill luck, and reporting three rhinoceroses a thousand years old who have loved sweet-gum oil since they were young.
91.4 avoid-cold-heat-dust | Cold-Avoiding, Heat-Avoiding, Dust-Avoiding | mysterious-yin-cave | wukong, three-rhinoceros-demons | Three banners with three names, and behind them ox-headed spirits of mountain, water and yellow cattle. A hundred and fifty rounds to a draw and a flag waved, and Bajie's only comment afterwards is what rhinoceros horn is worth by weight.

## CH 92  三僧大戰青龍山 四星挾捉犀牛怪
92.1 the-firefly | A Firefly in the First Month | mysterious-yin-cave | wukong, tripitaka | The master sees a light and says the season is wrong for fireflies — the insects have only just begun to stir — and it answers him in his disciple's voice.
92.2 the-broken-escape | Out Through the Gongs | mysterious-yin-cave | wukong, tripitaka, three-rhinoceros-demons | The lock comes open under his hand and they are nearly out when the demon king wakes the watch. Wukong fights out through several doors alone and the master is chained again behind him.
92.3 the-four-wood-stars | The Four Wood Beasts | mysterious-yin-cave | wukong, four-wood-stars, three-rhinoceros-demons | The Gold Star names them: rhinoceroses of seven kinds, one horn, three hairs, two horns, that part water and love to be clean. Their masters are the four wood-beast lodges, and the sight of them alone puts three demon kings back on four hooves.
92.4 into-the-western-sea | Horns That Part the Water | western-sea | wukong, moang, four-wood-stars, three-rhinoceros-demons | Their horns open a path through the sea and they run down it; the West Sea Dragon King's son brings out shrimp and crab soldiers to block the far end. Wukong calls for them alive, and is a moment too late for one of them.
92.5 the-four-horns | Four Horns and a Lifted Tax | jinping-prefecture | wukong, zhu-bajie, jinping-prefect | Four horns go to Heaven with the star officers as tribute, one stays in the prefecture treasury as the standing proof that no lamp oil is owed, and one goes west as an offering. The oil levy on two hundred and forty households is cancelled by proclamation.
92.6 leaving-before-dawn | Slipping Out Before Dawn | jinping-prefecture | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie | A month of banquets and Bajie has taken thirty-odd of them. The master has them saddle up in the dark and open the gate quietly, so as not to wake the monks or be invited to another.

## CH 93  給孤園問古談因 天竺國朝王遇偶
93.1 he-understands-it | He Understands It Without Words | jetavana-monastery-road | wukong, tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | Wukong says his master recites the Heart Sutra but has never had it explained, and that he can explain it — and then both of them fall silent, and the master tells the others that a silence is the real explanation.
93.2 gold-spread-monastery | The Ground Bought With Gold | jetavana-monastery | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, bujin-monks | The Jetavana, whose price was the ground covered in gold bricks; behind the monastery the foundations still wash up gold and silver beads after heavy rain. Bajie proposes taking a brick home for someone.
93.3 the-girl-in-the-cell | A Girl Kept in a Walled-Up Room | jetavana-monastery | tripitaka, wukong, hundred-year-abbot | A year ago tonight a wind put a girl on the old foundation who said she was the princess of India, blown from a moonlit garden. He walled her into a room with a hole for a bowl and told the monastery she was a demon; she understood, and has feigned madness since to be left alone.
93.4 the-embroidered-ball | The Ball Falls on a Monk | tianzhu-capital | tripitaka, wukong, jade-hare | Twenty years old and choosing a husband from a tower by throwing a ball into a crowd — and it is aimed. It knocks the pilgrim's hat crooked and rolls into his sleeve, and Wukong grows three fathoms tall to keep the crowd off it.
93.5 marry-and-find-out | Marry Her and Find Out | tianzhu-capital | wukong, tripitaka, tianzhu-king | The plan is to let the wedding proceed: agree, ask that his disciples be summoned to take their leave, and let one of them stand close enough to see what she is. Refusing means the executioner, and settles nothing about the girl in the cell.

## CH 94  四僧宴樂御花園 一怪空懷情慾喜
94.1 a-seat-for-the-bridegroom | A Son-in-Law Should Not Stand | tianzhu-capital | wukong, tripitaka, tianzhu-king | Wukong shouts at the king across the throne room for leaving his own son-in-law standing among the courtiers, and an embroidered stool is fetched.
94.2 four-screens-of-verse | Four Seasons on a Gold Screen | imperial-garden | tripitaka, tianzhu-king | Spring, summer, autumn and winter painted with poems by court scholars, and the king asks his son-in-law to match the rhymes. He does, in four, and the king has them set to music the same day.
94.3 send-the-disciples-away | She Cannot Bear to See Them | tianzhu-capital | jade-hare, tianzhu-king, wukong | The princess asks that the three ugly disciples be sent out of the city before the wedding, for fear of the sight of them. It is the last thing she does before the one who wanted to see her face gets close enough.
94.4 the-bee-on-the-hat | A Bee on the Bishop's Cap | tianzhu-capital | wukong, tripitaka | Gold and silver taken, farewells made in front of the whole court, and Wukong squeezing his master's hand as he goes. A hair keeps his place at the hostel; the real one comes back through the palace window as a bee and speaks from the brim of the hat.

## CH 95  假合真形擒玉兔 真陰歸正會靈元
95.1 a-wisp-of-demon-air | A Thread of Demon Air | tianzhu-capital | wukong, jade-hare, tripitaka | From the hat brim he sees it at last: a thin trace above her head, and not a very fierce one. His master asks him to wait until the king and queen withdraw, and he does not wait.
95.2 the-pestle | A Medicine Pestle for a Weapon | west-heaven-gate | wukong, jade-hare | Thick at one end and thin at the other, like the head of a rice-mortar. She names it, and in naming it names herself — and calls him Bimawen, which is the one thing that makes him swing without listening.
95.3 the-jade-hare | The Moon's Own Hare | maoying-mountain | wukong, taiyin-xingjun, jade-hare | The Star Lord of the Moon comes down to save her life: the hare that pounds the elixir of dark frost, gone a year, out through the jade lock. And the princess is the moon maiden Su'e, who slapped that hare eighteen years ago and went down to be born in a queen's belly.
95.4 the-locked-room-opened | The Door in the Back Wall | jetavana-monastery | tianzhu-king, tripitaka, real-princess, hundred-year-abbot | The king who has never been outside his own city walls rides sixty li and has the iron lock struck off, and holds a filthy, raving daughter without minding the filth. The abbot is made Monk Official of the Realm and the monastery given a name by imperial grant.
95.5 a-thousand-cockerels | A Thousand Cockerels for the Centipedes | baihua-mountain | wukong, tianzhu-king | Wukong's last request is practical: centipedes fear cockerels, so a thousand of the biggest are loosed on Hundred-Foot Mountain, and its name is changed to Precious Flower Mountain.

## CH 96  寇員外喜待高僧 唐長老不貪富貴
96.1 ten-thousand-monks-not-refused | Nine Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Six | kou-mansion | tripitaka, kou-hong | A tiger-seated gate with a board saying no monk is turned away. He is sixty-four, vowed at forty to feed ten thousand monks, and has a ledger: four short, and four arrive.
96.2 the-argument-about-leaving | Half a Month, and Another Half | kou-mansion | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, kou-hong, kou-wife | The wife offers her needlework money for another fortnight and the two sons their school allowance for a third; Bajie argues for all of it, is punched by Wukong, and slaps his own mouth. The master's answer is that he told an emperor three years and it has been fourteen.
96.3 ten-li-of-farewell | Banners, Drums, Monks and Daoists | kou-mansion | kou-hong, tripitaka | Two hundred invitations, twenty pairs of coloured banners, a band, a company of monks and a company of Daoists, and the whole prefecture out to watch. At the ten-li pavilion he weeps and asks them to come back through on the way home.
96.4 the-ruined-shrine | A Night in the Huaguang Shrine | huaguang-shrine | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | Fifty li on, a collapsed archway with a dusty board: the shrine of Huaguang, demoted for putting down the Poison-Fire Demon King. No caretaker, no roof worth the name, and rain all night — sitting up in the dry corners without speaking, so as not to be heard.

## CH 97  金酬外護遭魔蟄 聖顯幽魂救本原
97.1 the-robbery | The Send-Off That Drew the Thieves | kou-mansion | kou-hong, robbers | The banners and the drums told the whole city which house was richest. The same night, in the rain, a dozen men who had gambled their inheritances away come over the wall, and the old man who asks them to leave him something to be buried in is kicked to death.
97.2 the-widows-accusation | Four Names Read Off in the Dark | kou-mansion | kou-wife, kou-liang, kou-dong | From under the bed she says she saw them by the lamp: Tripitaka holding the torch, Bajie the knife, Sha Wujing carrying out the silver, and Sun Wukong the one who kicked her husband. Her sons believe her because she was the only one who looked.
97.3 the-bandits-again | The Same Bandits, Twice in One Day | west-road | wukong, tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing, robbers | On the road the thieves stop the very monks they robbed the house of, and Wukong plays the party's book-keeper before freezing all thirty. Hearing whose goods they are, Tripitaka insists on carrying the whole load back to the Kou house.
97.4 walking-into-it | Carrying the Evidence Home | tongtai-prefecture | tripitaka, wukong, tongtai-prefect | They meet the prefect's hundred and fifty constables on the road with the stolen property loaded on the horse. Wukong offers his own head to the head-clamp and breaks three cords with it, and the cassock buys off the jailers.
97.5 three-hauntings-in-one-night | Three Visits Before Dawn | tongtai-prefecture | wukong, kou-wife, tongtai-prefect | A voice from inside a coffin that calls the widow by her childhood name; an uncle's portrait on a wall that speaks to a nephew at his morning incense; and a foot the size of a courtroom lowered out of the sky over the county bench.
97.6 fetching-him-back | Fetched Back From Ksitigarbha | underworld | wukong, kou-hong, dizang | No runner ever summoned him; he walked in on his own and Ksitigarbha made him keeper of the ledger of good deeds. Wukong asks for him back, and gets him and twelve more years of life, and carries him home breathed into a sleeve.
97.7 out-of-the-coffin | Climbing Out of His Own Coffin | kou-mansion | kou-hong, tripitaka, tongtai-prefect | He climbs out, kowtows to the four of them, and then asks the officials what they are doing in his house. Then he calls his wife over and asks her, in front of the prefect, which of these men kicked him.

## CH 98  猿熟馬馴方脫殼 功成行滿見真如
98.1 the-golden-topped-immortal | The Immortal Who Waited Ten Years | jade-truth-temple | tripitaka, wukong, golden-topped-immortal | Guanyin told him ten years ago to expect them in two or three. He has watched the road every year since, and greets them with the complaint that he was misled.
98.2 the-real-road | The Cloud Road and the Ground Road | vulture-peak | wukong, golden-topped-immortal, tripitaka | Wukong says he knows the way; the immortal says he knows the cloud way, and a man who has not yet earned the clouds must walk the path — which does not leave by the temple's front gate but through the back of its own main hall.
98.3 the-single-log-bridge | A Single Log Over the Cloud-Reaching Ford | cloud-reaching-ford | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | Eight or nine li of water and one round log across it. Wukong runs it and back; Bajie lies down on the bank and asks to be allowed to fly, and is told that is exactly why he cannot.
98.4 the-bottomless-boat | A Boat With No Bottom | cloud-reaching-ford | tripitaka, wukong, jieyin-buddha | The ferryman is the Buddha Who Leads Across, and his boat has no bottom. Wukong pushes his master aboard from behind, and a corpse comes floating down the current — and all four of them, and the ferryman, tell him whose it is.
98.5 the-wordless-scriptures | Blank Paper for No Present | vulture-peak | ananda, kasyapa, tripitaka, wukong | Ananda and Kasyapa ask what gift has been brought for them, and are told there is none. The scrolls loaded on the horse are white from end to end, and it takes a snatched-away bundle and a scattering wind before anyone opens one.
98.6 three-pecks-of-gold | Sold Too Cheap Already | vulture-peak | buddha, tripitaka, wukong | The Buddha is not angry: scripture is not given free. Monks once recited these at a rich man's house in Sravasti for three pecks and three pints of gold, and he told them they had sold it too cheap. The wordless scrolls, he adds, were the true ones — but the east is not ready for them.
98.7 the-alms-bowl | The Emperor's Golden Bowl | vulture-peak | tripitaka, sha-wujing, ananda | The only thing they own worth giving is the purple-gold begging bowl the Tang emperor put into his hands at the gate of Chang'an, and it is handed over for the scriptures that have words in them.
98.8 five-thousand-and-forty-eight | Five Thousand and Forty-Eight Scrolls | vulture-peak | tripitaka, wukong, buddha, ananda | Every scroll opened and checked this time. The count is one canon's worth exactly, loaded on the horse and one carrying-pole, and the Buddha's charge is that nobody open a volume without bathing and fasting first.
98.9 eight-days-short | Eight Days Short of the Number | vulture-peak | guanyin, buddha | Guanyin returns the golden decree and points out the arithmetic: fourteen years is five thousand and forty days, and the canon is five thousand and forty-eight. Eight vajras are sent to carry them east and back inside eight days.

## CH 99  九九數完魔剗盡 三三行滿道歸根
99.1 the-ledger-of-ordeals | Eighty Ordeals, and One Short | potalaka | guanyin, jin-tou-jiedi | The guardians hand over the register they have kept the whole way. Guanyin reads it through and counts: nine times nine is the number that completes, and he has had eighty. One more is ordered, and a guardian is sent to catch the vajras up.
99.2 the-turtle-asks | The Question He Never Asked | tongtian-river | tripitaka, old-turtle, wukong | Eight years the turtle has waited at the bank for his answer. In four days on Vulture Peak the monk thought only about scriptures. He says nothing, and the turtle knows, and dips.
99.3 the-scriptures-dried | Drying the Scriptures on a Rock | tongtian-river | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | A night of wind, thunder and dark trying to take the sutras back, and then a morning spent laying them out on a boulder. The last scroll of the Buddha-Life sutra sticks to the stone and tears — and Wukong says heaven and earth are themselves incomplete, so the canon may as well be.
99.4 slipping-away-again | Leaving the Chens by Night | chen-village | tripitaka, wukong, chen-cheng, chen-qing | A rescue temple built in their name with four statues in it, Chen Guanbao and Yi-cheng-jin grown up and kowtowing, and dinner invitations to the end of the street. They go over the lock at the third watch, and the vajras call them up out of the yard.

## CH 100  徑回東土 五聖成真
100.1 the-pine-branches | The Pines Turned East | changan | tripitaka, taizong, hongfu-monks | Fourteen years ago he told his disciples that when the pines at Hongfu Temple turned their heads east he would be coming back. On this morning they have, and the monks are out of the gate before the news is.
100.2 the-passport-returned | Twelve Seals on a Passport | changan | tripitaka, taizong | Issued in the thirteenth year of Zhenguan; handed back in the twenty-seventh. Taizong reads the stamps down the page: Precious Elephant, Wuji, Cart-Slow, the Women's Country, Jisai, Purple Cinnabar, Bhiksu, Dharma-Respecting, and the four prefectures after them.
100.3 the-sacred-preface | A Preface Composed in the Night | changan | taizong, tripitaka | The emperor sits up all night and dictates it in the morning to a clerk: the Preface to the Sacred Teaching, and then apologises to the monk for the quality of it.
100.4 taken-back-mid-sentence | Called Away From the Reading Desk | dayan-pagoda | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing, eight-vajras | Copies commissioned, the high seat built, the sutra open at the first page — and the vajras call down through the incense to put it back down and come west. The court is left bowing at an empty platform.
100.5 five-holy-ones | Buddha, Buddha, Server, Arhat, Dragon | vulture-peak | buddha, tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing, white-dragon-horse | Sandalwood Merit Buddha; Victorious in Strife Buddha; Cleanser of Altars, over Bajie's loud objection and the Buddha's answer that it is a post with something in it; Golden Body Arhat; and the horse into the Dragon-Transforming Pool and up onto a pillar of the gate.
100.6 the-fillet-is-gone | Say the Loosening Spell | vulture-peak | wukong, tripitaka | The first thing he asks as a Buddha is to have the band taken off his head, and never used on anyone again. His master tells him to reach up and feel for it, and it is not there.
