46.1 meditation-tower | Fifty Tables High | cart-slow-kingdom | tripitaka, wukong, three-daoist-immortals | Tiger Power will not let the pass go and calls a new wager: cloud-ladder meditation, fifty tables stacked into a seat, no hands, no ladder, and no moving for hours.

46.5 the-three-beasts | A Tiger, a Deer and a Goat | cart-slow-kingdom | tripitaka, wukong | What is dragged from the execution ground is a headless yellow tiger, a white antlered deer, and a goat boiled to the bone — and a king weeping over an empty throne room learns the monks he enslaved were right.

47.1 eight-hundred-li | A Stone That Says How Wide | tongtian-river | tripitaka, wukong | Not a fisherman on the bank but a stele: Heaven-Reaching River, eight hundred li across, few have crossed it since antiquity. Even fiery eyes cannot see the far shore.

47.3 two-in-the-trays | Two Monks on Red Trays | chen-village | wukong, zhu-bajie | Wukong takes the boy's shape and Bajie, after two tries and one dropped spell, the girl's. Carried out on lacquer trays to gongs and torches, Bajie's only question is which the thing eats first.

## CH 48  魔弄寒風飄大雪 僧思拜佛履層冰

48.2 the-mandarin-fishs-plan | A Mandarin Fish's Plan | tongtian-river-palace | great-king-of-miraculous-response, mandarin-fish-crone | The spotted mandarin-fish crone asks whether he can make snow and ice, and lays out the whole trap: freeze eight hundred li, put walkers on it, and open the ice under the horse.
48.3 crossing-the-ice | Straw on the Hooves, Staffs Across the Shoulders | tongtian-river | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, wukong, sha-wujing | Traders cross to the Women's Country for the profit, so a monk crosses for a deadline. 48.4 chen-to-the-bottom | Chen All-the-Way-Down | tongtian-river | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing, wukong | The ice splits under them at the second day's dawn. Tripitaka goes into a six-foot stone chest behind the water palace, and Bajie reports his master's new name to the Chens.

## CH 49  三藏有災沉水宅 觀音救難現魚籃

49.2 the-stone-chest | Weeping in a Stone Chest | tongtian-river-palace | tripitaka, wukong | A long-legged shrimp-wife points him past the hall to a thing like a pig trough or a coffin, six feet long, with his master crying inside it.
49.3 the-door-sealed | The Door Packed With Mud | tongtian-river-palace | zhu-bajie, sha-wujing, great-king-of-miraculous-response | Once the mandarin-fish crone names the monkey, the demon simply stops coming out. Behind the broken door there is nothing but rammed earth and stone a thousand layers deep, and no fight to be had.
49.4 the-fish-basket | Guanyin Unbraided, Weaving | potalaka | wukong, guanyin | She comes out of the bamboo grove undressed, unpainted, carrying a basket she has just split and woven herself: the demon is her own goldfish from the lotus pond, and the copper mace an unopened lotus bud.
49.5 fish-basket-guanyin | The Painting They Made of Her | tongtian-river | guanyin, wukong, zhu-bajie | The whole village kneels in the mud to see her, and a painter among them takes down her likeness. That is where the Fish-Basket Guanyin comes from.
49.6 the-old-turtle | The Turtle Who Wants a Question Asked | tongtian-river | tripitaka, wukong, old-turtle | Nine years ago the fish took his house; now he wants to carry them across for it, and asks one payment — that at the Western Heaven they ask the Buddha when he will be let out of his shell.

## CH 50  情亂性從因愛慾 神昏心動遇魔頭
50.1 the-drawn-circle | A Circle Stronger Than Iron Walls | jindou-mountain | wukong, tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | The towers in the valley are a mirage; Wukong draws a ring on the ground with the cudgel and says it will hold off anything, only do not step out of it.
50.2 out-of-the-circle | Bajie Calls It a Prison | jindou-mountain | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | Cold feet and an argument about drawing a jail on the ground, and the master agrees. The gate they walk into is real enough to tie a horse to.
50.3 three-waistcoats | Three Brocade Waistcoats on a Table | jindou-cave | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | Behind the yellow curtain a skeleton with a skull the size of a peck-measure; on the table three lined waistcoats. 50.4 the-white-ring | A Ring That Takes the Cudgel | jindou-cave | wukong, single-horn-rhinoceros-king | Thirty rounds even, and the demon compliments his staffwork. 51.2 six-weapons-gone | Six Weapons and a Fire Department | jindou-mountain | wukong, nezha, li-jing, fire-star-lord | Nezha's six arms and six weapons multiply to ten thousand and the ring takes them all. ## CH 52  悟空大鬧金兜洞 如來暗示主人公
52.1 the-fire-let-loose | Fifty Monkeys and a Fire Dragon | jindou-cave | wukong, single-horn-rhinoceros-king | The bundle of his own hair is on a bamboo tray behind the door. 52.2 the-buddha-will-not-say | The Buddha Knows and Will Not Tell | vulture-peak | wukong, buddha | He knows the demon and refuses to name him: say it, and the thing will come to Vulture Peak and it will be the Buddha's trouble. 52.4 the-nose-ring | The Ring Through the Ox's Nose | jindou-mountain | laozi, wukong, single-horn-rhinoceros-king | One wave of the plantain fan and the demon's strength goes; the second shows a green ox. Laozi breathes on the bracelet, runs it through the nose, and rides him home — which is where the nose-ring comes from.

## CH 53  禪主吞餐懷鬼孕 黃婆運水解邪胎
53.1 the-ferrywoman | A Ferry Rowed by a Woman | mother-child-river | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, sha-wujing | She only smiles when asked where the ferryman is. On the far bank Tripitaka drinks half a bowl of the clear water and Bajie finishes it, and inside half an hour both are holding their bellies.
53.2 women-country-water | Twenty Years Old and Then the River | west-liang-women-country | tripitaka, zhu-bajie, wukong | The old woman laughs at them: this is the Women's Kingdom, the stream is the Motherhood River, and at the Male-Welcoming Post-house is a Reflection Pool that shows the double shadow. 53.3 abortion-spring | The Uncle of Red Boy | jieyang-mountain | wukong, ruyi-immortal | The Abortion Spring is held by the As-You-Will Immortal, brother of the Bull Demon King, who wants no offering but revenge for a nephew he thinks was killed — and does not believe he is a bodhisattva's attendant now.
53.4 the-bucket | Two Men for One Bucket | jieyang-mountain | wukong, sha-wujing, ruyi-immortal | Twice the as-you-will hook takes his ankle and drops the bucket down the well, so the third time is a decoy: Wukong fights at the slope while Sha Wujing draws the water. The hook is snapped into four pieces and its owner left alive.

## CH 54  法性西來逢女國 心猿定計脫煙花

## CH 56  神狂誅草寇 道迷放心猿
56.1 hung-from-a-tree | Hung in a Tree for a Toll | seven-hundred-li-slope | tripitaka, bandits | Thirty men with spears across the road; Tripitaka, who cannot lie, tells them his disciple is carrying silver. It is the first time he has bought his own safety with someone else's fist.
56.2 a-needle-for-a-toll | An Embroidery Needle for the Toll | seven-hundred-li-slope | wukong, bandits | Sixty blows on his skull, and he thanks them for the compliment. He offers the needle, plants the cudgel in the ground, invites them to lift it — and kills the two chiefs with it.
56.3 the-graveside-prayer | The Prayer That Named Him | seven-hundred-li-slope | tripitaka, wukong, zhu-bajie | Over the grave Tripitaka prays that the dead men sue Sun Wukong in the underworld and leave him out of it. 56.4 the-yang-farm | Sleeping in the Robbers' House | yang-farm | tripitaka, yang-laoer | Old Yang is seventy-four and has one son, who has been out five days robbing and burning. 56.5 the-severed-head | A Head Carried to the Horse | seven-hundred-li-slope | wukong, tripitaka | The pursuers are scattered at sunrise, and Wukong asks a wounded man which one is old Yang's son, cuts the head off, and brings it up to the saddle to be thanked. The spell is recited a dozen times, and this time the dismissal holds.

57.2 the-blow-on-the-back | A Cup of Water and a Blow | south-mountain-road | tripitaka, false-wukong | Alone and parched, the master is offered a cup of cool water and refuses it. The disciple's face changes, the cudgel comes down across his back, and the two blue-felt bundles go off into the sky.

57.4 four-days-at-the-throne | He Has Not Left This Throne in Four Days | potalaka | guanyin, wukong, sha-wujing | Sha Wujing swings his staff at the monkey standing beside the bodhisattva, and is told the thing he is accusing has been standing there four days.
